The useful intersection is **choosing visual or action-contingency observations to examine the agent's own predictive partition**. It requires separate definitions of observable dependence, monitoring, query control, access to alternative representations and independent prediction. A successful query can identify differences between stipulated channels. It cannot distinguish labels whose complete observation laws are equal, establish an ontological self/world boundary or measure human metacognitive ability.

## Five claims and their primary anchors

### 1. Monitoring and control are distinct operations

Thomas O. Nelson and Louis Narens (1990), *Metamemory: A Theoretical Framework and New Findings*, *Psychology of Learning and Motivation* 26, 125–173; [DOI 10.1016/S0079-7421(08)60053-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0079-7421(08)60053-5). The [author institutional scan](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~lnarens/1990/Nelson%26Narens_Book_Chapter_1990.pdf) was retrieved in full; targeted inspection covered printed pp. 125–130, principles 1–3, Fig. 1, control, monitoring and introspection. The chapter's empirical experiments were not reanalyzed.

The meta-level maintains a model of object-level processes. Monitoring informs that model; control changes object-level processing. Introspective reports can be imperfect and should be explained as data rather than assumed to reveal internal mechanisms accurately. This supports separating a diagnostic posterior or monitoring record from the policy that chooses a query. Merely computing ordinary scene uncertainty does not establish a separate metacognitive process. TFN would supply its own finite operationalization, not reproduce the chapter's human memory experiment.

### 2. Posterior confidence is not metacognitive sensitivity

Stephen M. Fleming and Hakwan C. Lau (2014), *How to measure metacognition*, *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* 8:443; [DOI 10.3389/fnhum.2014.00443](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00443). Retrieved [Europe PMC full-text XML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4097944/fullTextXML). Inspected introduction/Fig. 1, confidence–accuracy table, ROC analysis, efficiency, bias and probability-judgment sections. This is a methodological review, used as the authors' explicit measurement framework rather than as a new intervention experiment.

Mean confidence, discrimination of correct versus incorrect judgments, and efficiency conditional on first-order performance differ. A posterior entropy or maximum posterior probability is therefore an uncertainty/confidence descriptor. Without trialwise first-order judgments and corresponding metacognitive reports, neither is a measured human sensitivity or efficiency endpoint. For a synthetic study, report candidate prediction and calibration explicitly. Even calibrated Bayesian confidence can be confidently wrong under model misspecification; a well-specified posterior should not be called learned introspective skill.

### 3. A metacognitive attention architecture is an explicit modeling commitment

Lars Sandved-Smith, Casper Hesp, Jérémie Mattout, Karl Friston, Antoine Lutz and Maxwell J. D. Ramstead (2021), *Towards a computational phenomenology of mental action: modelling meta-awareness and attentional control with deep parametric active inference*, *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2021(1):niab018; [DOI 10.1093/nc/niab018](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niab018). Higher-level states condition lower-level likelihood precision; second-level policies change attentional states, while a third level represents meta-awareness. This is an implemented simulation architecture and a precedent for representing one's inference processes. Its authors explicitly set aside selective attention in epistemic foraging and leave empirical fitting to future work. TFN's information-gain query policy would be a different, simpler construction. A binary access gate or query bonus is not a reproduction of the three-level architecture or proof of phenomenological access.

### 4. Task-directed gaze changes sampling, but does not by itself establish metacognition

Monica S. Castelhano, Michael L. Mack and John M. Henderson (2009), *Viewing task influences eye movement control during active scene perception*, *Journal of Vision* 9(3):6, 1–15; [DOI 10.1167/9.3.6](https://doi.org/10.1167/9.3.6). Fresh inspection used the already preserved Edinburgh institutional publisher PDF, not its historical verification claims. Read Methods, task performance, whole-scene/spatial analyses and Discussion.

Twenty participants performed counterbalanced memorization and visual search. Critical search scenes omitted the target; target-present fillers encouraged continued search. The paper reports task-dependent scene-region selection and aggregate viewing measures, while individual fixation duration and overall average saccade amplitude did not show the same task dependence. Its Discussion nevertheless identifies an initial-viewing saccade-amplitude difference: overall averages do not establish invariance at every viewing phase. It supports treating task instructions as part of the sampling mechanism. It does not support the inference that any altered scan path reflects metacognitive access, gratitude or boundary understanding. TFN's location indices and unit-square distances lack the apparatus, temporal resolution and visual-angle interpretation of these human observations.

## A defensible intersection design and its controls

The following is an authored mathematical proposal, not a result extracted from a human study. Let Z identify a **predictive structure**, and let a query q expose a specified observation O. Use a declared channel P(O|Z,q), current supported posterior p(Z|history), and EIG(q) = I(Z;O|history,q). Object-level gaze asks about scene content; a proposed metalevel operation monitors the model's structure uncertainty and selects a query relevant to that representation. If the code contains only flat state inference, call the experiment structural inquiry rather than claiming the full metacognitive architecture above.

A small independently enumerated fixture has joint outcomes 00,01,10,11 and two channels:

| Outcome | Z=0 | Z=1 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 00 | .45 | .05 |
| 01 | .05 | .45 |
| 10 | .05 | .45 |
| 11 | .45 | .05 |

Both individual bits have probability .5 under either candidate. Under equal candidate priors, observing the pair has information `.3680642071684971` nats; a single bit gives zero information. Independent expected-posterior-entropy reduction matches the joint information formula. Replacing the second column by the first gives exactly zero information. These are recorded numerical fixtures in `pinned-api-probe.json`, not the eventual TFN channel selection or cohort result.

The experiment must define whether two separately sampled bits come from the **same event**. Seeing both coordinates of one latent event reveals a joint sample; independent samples from different events need not reveal within-event dependence. If previous single-bit observations can be paired afterward, an apparent marginal-only comparator may already possess joint information. Pair access and exposure identifiers must therefore be explicit, including whether a joint query costs two observations. An action-contingency variant similarly needs an observed/randomized action and a declared conditional channel; observational association alone is not a general causal-identification guarantee.

Use a true label-equivalence control: if two candidates have equal observation laws for **every allowed query and observable history**, posterior odds between them remain their prior odds after every update. They need not be 50:50. Adaptive query selection based only on observed history cannot remove this equality. Increasing independent synthetic N reduces Monte Carlo uncertainty and can improve identification of distinguishable classes; it cannot distinguish members of an observational equivalence class. A policy must not access the true candidate through hidden metadata or label-specific costs.

| Proposed scenario | Required observable and independent control |
| --- | --- |
| Informative joint query versus marginal query | Exactly equal single-bit likelihood columns; distinct normalized joint columns; EIG equals expected posterior KL/entropy reduction. Include a normalized perturbation that breaks the marginal equality and must be detected. |
| Joint opportunity versus extra sample budget | Compare equal primitive-observation and action-cost budgets. Report exposure counts separately from independent agents. |
| Label equivalence under larger N | Posterior odds unchanged at each history, not merely a nonsignificant group contrast. Consistent candidate permutation preserves predictions, evidence and policies after reversing the map. |
| Monitoring/control separation | Record structure posterior/uncertainty before action; policy depends only on available history. A monitoring-only condition may calculate the same posterior without selecting diagnostic queries. |
| Access blocked, sham or matched random queries | Maintain costs/activity as appropriate; remove diagnostic information or strategic allocation explicitly. Distinguish access benefits from query-policy benefits. |
| Helpful prior, contradicted prior and misspecification | Include useful-structure retention, omitted candidate and identical-channel contexts. Permit no advantage or harm; a gate must not require a favorable policy result. |
| Independent predictive value | Score future or independently sampled observations on a common target distribution, not just selected locations or the data used to choose the partition. |
| Cohort enlargement | Resample complete paired independent agents/replicates within declared contexts. Fixations, channels and repeated windows are not additional subjects. Keep exploratory families and exact-null status explicit. |

These controls remain implementation recommendations. The current research probe established only the small fixture identities, imported API behavior, explicit invalid-input rejection and the narrow Gaussian comparator result.

### Checks specific to the integrator's proposed ternary design

The integrator subsequently proposed latent scene Z and reference encoding H, each with three values, appearance W=(Z+H) mod 3, and probes exposing randomized action a and response r=(a+H) mod 3 through declared noisy channels. These are design communications, not inspected implementation or executed results. Three checks follow:

1. Scene-only evidence preserves the prior over H when Z is uniform and independent of H and the observation/query process respects the resulting symmetry. Verify this against the admitted prior and update logic, including restricted support. Matched individual marginals alone do not establish the posterior statement for arbitrary joint priors.
2. An assessment target anchored at H=0 is a stipulated reference-frame intervention or transfer task. Its score can test whether the model uses inferred W and H together to predict Z. It does not measure ordinary future prediction under the original unchanged observation process or the truth of an agent boundary.
3. Erasing a makes the probe response marginal uninformative under a uniform independent action. However, a sham that receives the integrated agent's adaptive query schedule may receive information through those query choices. Prove the schedule's relevant invariance, or identify the comparator as a nonautonomous matched-query construction that ignores schedule information. Do not infer complete information erasure from response-marginal equality alone.
