## Gratitude is a family of constructs, not one precision parameter

The interpersonal account of gratitude emphasizes recognizing another’s moral action, motivating reciprocity and reinforcing generosity ([McCullough et al., 2001](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.127.2.249)). The relational account emphasizes recognizing and maintaining responsive relationships ([Algoe, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1751-9004.2012.00439.x)). A broader review also includes habitual appreciation of positive aspects of life ([Wood et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2010.03.005)). These accounts motivate different measurements: benefactor recognition, interpersonal behavior, and general appreciation cannot be assumed interchangeable.

This matters for “nothingness.” Appreciation that a harm is absent may have no benefactor; relief can occur without gratitude; and an unobserved outcome may convey no favorable evidence at all. [Yu et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2944-17.2018) explicitly separate recipient benefit from benefactor cost in a social pain-reduction task. Their abstract provides a useful construct distinction, but its neural results cannot be imported into a model lacking those measured variables. Nor is gratitude necessarily uncomplicated positive affect: [He et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.824326) report a selected indebtedness contrast following interpersonal gratitude writing, but full-text inspection shows a non-significant omnibus indebtedness test (p = .077) and a no-writing control. This adolescent experiment motivates separate measures of burden, obligation and appreciation without establishing universal effects.

## Intervention evidence: positive findings coexist with demanding comparators

The catalog retains the foundational [counting-blessings experiment](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.84.2.377) as metadata-verified context, without deriving effect sizes from an unread full paper. The inspected abstracts and targeted full-text readings show why a single summary of “gratitude works” is inadequate. [Cunha et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00584) report that several outcomes changed similarly under gratitude and neutral-event writing. Their full-text discussion reports a positive-affect difference at post-test but no between-group differences in the assessed outcomes at follow-up. Their analysis includes only 410 of 1,337 randomized participants with post-baseline responses; neutral lists often contained positive events. [Heckendorf et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brat.2019.103415) report reduced repetitive negative thinking for a guided intervention versus a wait list; that design cannot isolate gratitude from guidance or expectancy. The [Hartanto et al. institutional abstract](https://smusg.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/a-critical-examination-of-the-effectiveness-of-gratitude-interven/) reports favorable within-person diary outcomes, so its critical title must not be mistaken for a null result.

Meta-analytic conclusions likewise depend on the question. [Davis et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1037/cou0000107) report no advantage over psychologically active comparisons for well-being. [Cregg and Cheavens](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10902-020-00236-6) report small symptom effects, rather than a basis for prescribing this simulation as treatment. [Kerry et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.2147/PRBM.S372432) highlight weak evidence of superiority over positively valenced controls and possible demand or placebo explanations. [Dickens (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1080/01973533.2017.1323638) is retained as a metadata-verified additional synthesis, without inventing its detailed findings.

A newer preregistered [cross-cultural meta-analysis by Choi et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2425193122) reports small aggregate gains and between-country variability, with methodological moderation and examined cultural moderators failing to explain that variability. This is useful counterweight to an exclusively skeptical narrative, but it does not validate a new absence-based intervention. Neither favorable aggregates nor older active-control concerns identify the source model’s precision mechanism. Future tests should therefore compare matched activities and expectancy, evaluate individual variation, and distinguish aggregate benefit from mechanism identification.

## Active inference supplies a model language and testable commitments

The gaze expansion adds the correctly identified [Yarbus complex-objects chapter](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4899-5379-7_8)
at metadata/abstract scope, and [Castelhano, Mack and Henderson's task experiment](https://doi.org/10.1167/9.3.6)
at targeted full-text scope. Task instructions changed scene-region selection;
that result neither tests gratitude nor licenses interpreting model occupancy as
measured fixation duration. Mirza's model sections were inspected in full text.
The third new catalog entry is the museum artwork record, retained for identity
and licensing rather than scientific effect evidence. The [gaze dossier](yarbus-research.md)
and copied receipt manifest preserve reading locators and access limitations.

The [process theory](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_00912), [discrete-state comparison](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01357), and [POMDP tutorial](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmp.2021.102632) give the computational lineage. In this project, categorical likelihoods, priors and transition tensors must be declared, normalized and admitted; Dirichlet counts must be distinguished from their probability means. Those implementation requirements are our explicit mathematical contract, not empirical findings about gratitude. Numerical agreement with an independent finite calculation establishes an algorithmic result only.

The [selective-attention model](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-50138-8) provides a rationale for context-sensitive precision changing which observations are informative to an agent. The [affect-congruent attention experiment](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011707) links manipulated affect, attention and reward expectations in its task. Targeted full-text inspection also shows that gaze findings differed by probe type and looking-time normalization; model attention weights and overt gaze were distinct measurements. The [valence model](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01341) offers a formal account of affective inference while explicitly motivating later empirical fitting. These are complementary contributions, not interchangeable evidence for automatic gratitude-induced gaze changes. The catalog also identifies the [gratitude-letter/attention study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10902-021-00377-2) and [interpretation-bias study](https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2021.1940249) as relevant candidates whose metadata alone cannot establish detailed effects here.

The source [preprint](https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/xm25y_v1) makes a strong positive-event reassignment during rehearsal. The [journal abstract](https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO.a.1591) describes an abstract attention proof of concept. Our source analysis retains that distinction: manipulating precision shows what the model can do under that intervention, not that its value was learned and transferred in humans.

## Optimism must be checked against calibration

The source cites [Sharot’s overview](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2011.10.030), retained here at metadata level. Primary abstracts provide a more contested picture of measurement. [Garrett et al. (2014)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00639) report differences in good/bad-news updating between clinical and comparison groups. [Hobbs et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.190814) replicate a negative-event group difference while finding positive-event results inconclusive. Neither result means that more optimism is always beneficial.

[Shah et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2016.05.004) argue that apparent updating asymmetries can arise from task and statistical artifacts. [Garrett and colleagues’ reply](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2016.10.013) disputes the critique’s confounds and simulation assumptions. Retaining both means the project must test its own estimator under unbiased agents, matched estimation errors, endpoint effects and balanced news conditions. A positive difference score cannot be its own validation criterion.

The [proper-scoring-rule paper](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437) is a metadata-verified methods reference. Our independently defined finite Brier calculation compares predictions to realized outcomes; an optimistic shift that worsens that score must remain visible. Brier loss measures predictive probability performance, not calibration alone; a calibration-specific interpretation requires an appropriate diagnostic or decomposition. [Newman et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0001025) further show in their observational abstract that gratitude and optimism have overlapping but distinct associations. The study is not a randomized gratitude treatment, despite its title’s use of “benefits.”

## Statistical methods: nominal levels need measured performance

The added methods sources identify the statistical lineage without treating a
citation as validation of this implementation. [Efron (1979)](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176344552)
is metadata-verified bootstrap context. The repository's specific percentile
interval is separately defined and tested. Calling an interval “95%” specifies
its nominal target; it does not establish 95% coverage in a finite simulation.
Coverage must be measured against the known generating parameter, with uncertainty
for that measured proportion. Undercoverage must remain visible rather than being
removed by changing the benchmark, seed, endpoint or acceptance threshold.

[Morris, White and Crowther (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8086) describe a structured approach, now inspected in the full-text estimand and performance sections, linking aims, generating
mechanisms, estimands, methods and performance measures. Applied here, those
roles require declaring the fixed luckiness grid, agent-level estimand and
comparison procedures before examining their operating characteristics. Each
whole paired agent is the resampling unit; days and time points are repeated
measurements within that unit. A synthetic Gaussian fixture checks a statistical
procedure on that fixture, not power or coverage in a human gratitude trial. Different world weights define different estimands; exploratory world contrasts should not silently expand the primary family of tests.

[Welch (1947)](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/34.1-2.28) and
[Satterthwaite (1946)](https://doi.org/10.2307/3002019) are metadata-verified
references for the lineage of an analytic variance/degree-of-freedom comparator.
They do not certify a project-specific stratified interval. Such a comparator
must state its independent-agent and variance assumptions and be assessed on the
same unchanged fixtures as the bootstrap. Adding a robustness comparison should
retain the original bootstrap estimates, coverage counts and limitations; it is
not a reason to replace an inconvenient result. No analytic-comparator coverage
result is asserted in this literature synthesis.

[Clopper and Pearson (1934)](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/26.4.404) provide
metadata-verified binomial-interval lineage. Here, uncertainty about a measured
coverage or rejection proportion is distinct from the confidence interval for a
study effect. The former concerns repeated simulation outcomes; neither is
evidence about the validity of a psychological mechanism.

[Phipson and Smyth (2010)](https://doi.org/10.2202/1544-6115.1585) warn in their
inspected deposited abstract about understated p-values from randomly sampled
permutations, especially when testing multiple hypotheses. The project explicitly
uses a plus-one finite-draw convention and reports its resolution and Monte Carlo
uncertainty. Its sign-flip null also requires symmetry/exchangeability of paired
differences: matching exposures does not establish that assumption. The
[Holm (1979) citation](https://www.jstor.org/stable/4615733) is verified from the
primary issue contents and supplies multiple-testing lineage; the adjustment is
independently implemented and tested here. A family-adjusted p-value does not
repair a biased estimator, a failed null assumption or poor interval coverage.

Finally, [Brier (1950)](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493%281950%29078%3C0001%3Avofeit%3E2.0.co%3B2)
is verified through publisher-deposited metadata and the primary AMS record;
no abstract or full-text interpretation is claimed. This repository defines its
one-component binary loss explicitly as `(q-y)^2`, so a direction of optimism
cannot substitute for predictive accuracy.

The retrieval audit corrected the supplied Satterthwaite identifier:
`10.2307/3002022` identifies Mood and Snedecor's *40.Query*, whereas the verified
Satterthwaite article is `10.2307/3002019`. The candidate Holm DOI returned no
Crossref record; the catalog therefore retains the verified JSTOR stable URL
and leaves its DOI unset. Failed responses and the bounded primary-metadata
observations are retained alongside the successful Crossref receipts. These
limits prevent bibliographic identity from being overstated as full-text review.

## Studentized intervals and diagnostic stress tests

The [Hall (1988) deposited abstract](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176350933) motivates comparing percentile-t methods, but its full text was not acquired. No theorem assumptions or finite-sample guarantee are imported from that abstract. The [official boot.ci manual](https://www.stat.math.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/boot/html/boot.ci.html), archived as version 1.3-32, requires variance estimates for both the original statistic and bootstrap replicates. This is the relevant contract for a studentized comparison; citing the manual does not demonstrate that the Python implementation meets it.

[White et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyad134), inspected in full-text diagnostic sections, recommend known-answer settings and explicit investigation of failures and outliers. For this project, original intervals and failed/undefined comparisons should remain visible. Coverage conditional on available intervals differs from coverage over all repetitions. Heavy-tailed, skew and heterogeneous mathematical fixtures can expose limits without becoming human trial evidence. A centered skew fixture need not satisfy a sign-flip test's symmetry assumption. The [revision memo](scholarship-revision.md) maps these sources to the proposed analysis and preserves precise access ceilings.

## Four meanings of “nothing”

| Meaning | Operational requirement | Relevant scholarship | Prohibited inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Observed absence | A detector or observation process returns an absence category; its reliability is specified | [Goh et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2301463120) report auditory silence illusions | Silence perception proves metaphysical emptiness |
| Missing evidence | Observation is unavailable; the likelihood update is explicitly omitted or the missingness process is modeled | [Rubin (1976)](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/63.3.581), metadata-verified methods context | No observation means no harm |
| Counterfactual avoided harm | Compare actual utility with an explicitly modeled alternative and retain uncertainty about that alternative | [Coricelli et al. (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1514), [Seymour et al. (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1527), [Koo et al. (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0013316) | A counterfactual scenario proves a harm was actually prevented |
| Model non-reification | Keep model assumptions and alternatives available for revision; define any finite surrogate precisely | [SN 12.15, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn12/sn12.015.than.html), [Garfield’s translation/commentary](https://academic.oup.com/book/50348/chapter-abstract/422713050), [Empty Words](https://academic.oup.com/book/49905) | Model uncertainty measures Buddhist realization |

The counterfactual sources concern different operations. Coricelli’s study uses information about unchosen gambles to induce regret; Seymour’s study examines learned expectation of pain relief; Koo’s studies mentally subtract positive life events. Coricelli and Seymour remain abstract-level here; the targeted Koo full-text inspection confirms a mental-subtraction manipulation rather than an absence detector. These sources motivate contrastive evaluations, but none tests this project’s combined “gratitude for nothingness” construction. A new finite avoided-harm quantity is therefore a conceptual extension with its own assumptions, not a recovered measure from those papers.

The inspected SN 12.15 translation rejects attachment to both existence and non-existence and presents dependent origination and cessation. That textual argument helps constrain the project’s metaphor: “nothingness” should not announce that nothing exists. The Garfield book’s title-page identity is verified, but its full commentary was not read; an anomalous publisher landing-page abstract describing Thomas Nagel was explicitly excluded. The *Empty Words* publisher synopsis supports the relevance of cross-cultural interpretation, not an asserted identity between Buddhist arguments and Bayesian inference. The newly inspected [MN 121 translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.121.than.html) explicitly acknowledges residual presence while describing absence and later treats refined concentration as fashioned and impermanent. It is a named English translation, not a Pali-critical or Madhyamaka analysis. Traditional texts, philosophical commentaries and finite software tests remain different kinds of evidence.

## What would change the evidence ceiling

The [v0.6 targeted dossier](scholarship-v060-research.md) adds inspected primary
texts on joint resampling and information demand. Romano-Wolf supplies the joint-
bootstrap lineage under explicit consistency/continuity conditions; the project
does not implement its stepdown algorithm. Hothorn and colleagues' author technical
report supplies covariance and positive-variance context; final journal layout
was not inspected. The [joint method](joint-uncertainty.md) defines the actual
procedure and retains measured undercoverage rather than borrowing a guarantee.

Kobayashi and Hsu distinguish instrumental and anticipatory information motives
in one actionable-lottery task. Van Lieshout and colleagues measure costly waiting
for noninstrumental information, with an uncertainty predictor that includes
payoff differences. DeSteno and colleagues' planned contrast supports patience
after gratitude recall within its immediate task. These sources sharpen rival
explanations and future measurements; none establishes appreciation-to-realization
mediation or identifies the project's utility parameters. Source bodies and DOI
metadata are retained with hashes under `data/scholarship/receipts/v060/`.

A useful computational result would show, under declared assumptions, that an absence observation, a missing observation, and a counterfactual comparison lead to different outputs; preserve null and reversal controls; and report paired uncertainty and calibration alongside positive expectation. That result would support software semantics and a synthetic mechanism, not a recommendation to practice gratitude in unsafe circumstances.

A future empirical program would separately measure appreciation, relief, gratitude toward a benefactor, indebtedness, perceived safety and well-being. It would use expectancy-matched active controls, explicit missing-data handling, preregistered outcomes, parameter recovery, held-out prediction, and an attention task capable of falsifying transfer. Unsafe environments and false reassurance are central boundary conditions, consistent with the source preprint’s P035 and P122. Establishing efficacy, neural mechanisms, clinical utility or contemplative attainment would require new data and an independent validation standard; no simulation in this repository supplies them.
