## Explain the collections before invoking the discourses

*Nikāya* means a collection here. The **Saṃyutta Nikāya** (SN), conventionally
rendered **Grouped Discourses** or **Connected Discourses**, organizes discourses
by related topics. SN 12.15 identifies discourse 15 within the twelfth connected
collection, on dependent origination. The **Majjhima Nikāya** (MN), **Middle-length
Discourses**, is a different collection; MN 121 is discourse 121. These are
collections of the Pali Sutta Piṭaka, not authors or statistical models.
The primary edition's [SN index](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/)
and [MN index](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/) supply this orientation;
receipts: `sn-collection.html`, `mn-collection.html`. SuttaCentral uses the English
collection labels Linked Discourses and Middle Discourses in the inspected
translation data. Explain the abbreviation once, then use SN 12.15 and MN 121.

### SN 12.15: the discourse to Kaccāna Gotta

The catalog's source is **Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu's 1997 translation**, *Kaccayanagotta
Sutta: To Kaccayana Gotta (on Right View)*, in the Access to Insight edition last
revised 30 November 2013. The discourse responds to a question about right view,
rejects the two extremes concerning existence, and presents conditioned arising
and cessation. This is a constraint on interpreting nothingness as annihilation,
not evidence that a Bayesian state space encodes dependent origination.

Recommended quotation, **14 whitespace-separated words**:

> 'Everything exists': That is one extreme. 'Everything doesn't exist': That is a second extreme.

Locator: fourth discourse paragraph, beginning with the two extremes, after the
paragraph on clinging and self; [ATI SN 12.15](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn12/sn12.015.than.html).
Receipt: `sn1215-ati.html`. This page has no numbered discourse paragraphs; the
paragraph count here starts with the opening narrative, excluding navigation and
editorial notices.

The [current dhammatalks version](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN12_15.html)
adds an introduction and translator's notes. Note 3 interprets the apparent
existence/nonexistence tension through modes of attending, rather than rival
ontological declarations. Attribute that interpretation to the translator; do not
silently present the note as ancient discourse. Receipt: `sn1215-current.html`.

### MN 121: a shorter discourse on emptiness

The catalog's source is **Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu's 1997 translation**, *Cula-suññata
Sutta: The Lesser Discourse on Emptiness*, ATI revision 30 November 2013. The
progression distinguishes absent disturbances from a remaining basis of
experience, and eventually examines refined concentration as conditioned and
subject to cessation. It is not a blank-state ideal or a prescription to erase
all distinctions.

Recommended quotation, **9 whitespace-separated words**:

> Whatever remains, he discerns as present: 'There is this.'

Locator: the final paragraph of the initial wilderness-perception sequence,
immediately before **The Perception of Earth**; [ATI MN 121](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.121.than.html).
Receipt: `mn121-ati.html`. The **Release** section provides the conditioned-state
qualification. The discourse's closing approval by Ānanda should not be relabeled
as a measured gratitude response or a gratitude-to-realization mechanism.

The [current dhammatalks version](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN121.html)
is titled *The Shorter Discourse on Emptiness* and revises the repeated affective
description toward confidence and resolve. This matters if discussing whether
the passage depicts pleasure or a particular emotion. Retain the edition-specific
quotation rather than mixing the 1997 text with revised wording. Receipt:
`mn121-current.html`; `ati-update.html` records the site's revision notice.

## Four substantive additions

### 1. Gaze can serve a changing task: Tatler and colleagues

**Benjamin W. Tatler, Mary M. Hayhoe, Michael F. Land and Dana H. Ballard (2011).**
*Eye guidance in natural vision: Reinterpreting salience.* **Journal of Vision**
11(5), article 5, pp. 1–23. DOI [10.1167/11.5.5](https://doi.org/10.1167/11.5.5).
[Author-hosted published PDF](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~dana/Tatler.pdf).

**Actual reading:** introduction (p. 1), evaluation of image-salience explanations
(pp. 2–3), and conclusions (pp. 17–18). Receipt: `tatler2011-author.pdf` and
`tatler2011-author.txt`; identity: `tatler2011-crossref.json`. Crossref's page
field is 5–5 because this journal uses article numbers; retain article 5 and the
PDF's 1–23 span, not an invented five-page range.

**Support and placement:** In `sec-boundary-interpretation`, extend the existing
Castelhano task finding with the authors' argument that gaze models need
behavioral relevance, learning and changing information requirements. A fixed
painting partition is an authored test environment, not a natural-vision model.

**Limit:** This is the authors' review and theoretical synthesis, not a new
gratitude intervention or empirical validation of this project's policy. Their
critique does not imply that low-level salience never matters. It supports asking
what the observer is doing and when information is needed.

### 2. Looking and knowing where one looked: Võ and colleagues

**Melissa L.-H. Võ, Avigael M. Aizenman and Jeremy M. Wolfe (2016).** *You think
you know where you looked? You better look again.* **Journal of Experimental
Psychology: Human Perception and Performance** 42(10), 1477–1481.
DOI [10.1037/xhp0000264](https://doi.org/10.1037/xhp0000264).
[Author manuscript in PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5079107/).

**Actual reading:** Methods/Participants, Procedure, Analysis, Results and
Discussion; extracted text lines 1035–1104. Receipts: `vo2016-pmc.html`,
`vo2016-pmc.txt`, `vo2016-crossref.json`. Eight observers per experiment marked
remembered fixation locations after three-second viewing; the comparison included
guesses about another observer and an idealized noisy-memory model.

**Support and placement:** At `sec-boundary-interpretation` and
`sec-empirical-boundary`, distinguish recorded gaze from retrospective knowledge
of gaze. Own-fixation reports offered little advantage over other-observer guesses;
the second experiment's own/other contrast was marginal (p = .07), not a robust
demonstration of privileged access.

**Limit:** Small, task-specific samples and retrospective reports do not show that
all online monitoring is absent, or that deliberate control cannot improve it.
This study does not test meditation. Its comparison motivates an independent
monitoring measure; gaze change alone cannot supply that measure.

### 3. Distinguish meta-awareness from dereification: Lutz and colleagues

**Antoine Lutz, Amishi P. Jha, John D. Dunne and Clifford D. Saron (2015).**
*Investigating the phenomenological matrix of mindfulness-related practices from
a neurocognitive perspective.* **American Psychologist** 70(7), 632–658.
DOI [10.1037/a0039585](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0039585).
[Author-hosted published PDF](https://www.johnddunne.net/uploads/9/8/5/6/9856107/lutzjhadunne___saron_investigating_the_phenomenological_matrix_of_mindfulness-related_practices_from_a_neurocognitive_perspective_american_psychologist_oct_2015_.pdf).

**Actual reading:** phenomenological-matrix introduction and functional dimensions
(printed pp. 637–642), concluding research directions (pp. 649–650).
Receipts: `lutz2015-author.pdf`, `lutz2015-author.txt`, `lutz2015-crossref.json`.

**Support and placement:** After the existing Dahl taxonomy in
`sec-enabling-conditions`, identify object orientation, dereification and
meta-awareness as distinct proposed dimensions. Their framework allows heightened
meta-awareness without dereification. This directly strengthens the distinction
between noticing a framing assumption and changing one's relationship to it.

**Limit:** The authors call the matrix heuristic and its placement of practices
provisional. Its dimensions are not a validated common scale, and their local
independence is a conceptual proposal, not evidence of statistical independence.
Neither introspective access nor flexible switching is thereby sufficient for
realization of emptiness. The paper supplies vocabulary and hypotheses, not an
efficacy estimate for gratitude.

### 4. Match the construct, instruction and measurement: Van Dam and colleagues

**Nicholas T. Van Dam, Marieke K. van Vugt, David R. Vago, Laura Schmalzl,
Clifford D. Saron, Andrew Olendzki, Ted Meissner, Sara W. Lazar, Catherine E. Kerr,
Jolie Gorchov, Kieran C. R. Fox, Brent A. Field, Willoughby B. Britton,
Julie A. Brefczynski-Lewis and David E. Meyer (2018).** *Mind the Hype: A Critical
Evaluation and Prescriptive Agenda for Research on Mindfulness and Meditation.*
**Perspectives on Psychological Science** 13(1), 36–61.
DOI [10.1177/1745691617709589](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691617709589).
[Author manuscript in PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5758421/).
Use the 2018 issue year; online-first publication was 10 October 2017.

**Actual reading:** II.B–II.C (semantic ambiguity and integrative assessment),
III.A (construct validity; self-report concerns and recommendations). Receipts:
`vandam2018-pmc.html`, `vandam2018-pmc.txt`, `vandam2018-crossref.json`.

**Support and placement:** In the empirical-design section, specify the practice
instructions and process each measure targets. The authors discuss changing
interpretations of questionnaire items, demand characteristics and the danger
of treating distinct practices as one intervention.

**Limit:** This is a critical methodological synthesis. It is not evidence that
all contemplative reports are invalid or all practices ineffective. The project
should triangulate reported experience, chosen inquiry and independent
performance without claiming any one constitutes a realization scale.

## Concise integration and supplement allocation

For the main text, expand the first emptiness paragraph into two short paragraphs:
introduce the collection names and discourse titles; embed the two recommended
short quotations with Ṭhānissaro and edition identification; explain the
absent/present distinction and the conditioned status of refined concentration.
Put the Sujato comparison and current translator-note distinctions in a technical
supplement or footnote. Avoid a long translation survey in the main argument.

The bridge to gratitude should remain explicitly the authors' proposal:

> Gratitude for realizing emptiness can mean appreciation for the conditions of
> inquiry, for an insight that changes how a distinction is held, and for the
> continuing availability of revision. These are different temporal relations:
> motivation before examination, appreciation following it, and support for its
> continuation. Our model executes a limited inquiry mechanism; it does not
> establish that any of these appreciations produces contemplative realization.

This passage is original synthesis, not a scriptural quotation. It offers a
positive object of appreciation without turning the absence of harm into proof
of insight or treating the manuscript's proposed mechanism as the discourses'
historical theory. Keep ordinary gratitude, interpersonal benefaction, relief,
appreciation of opportunity and appreciation following insight distinct in the
proposed human study.

At the gaze/structural-inquiry intersection, use one short paragraph distinguishing
four observations: where eyes move; what a participant knows about that movement;
whether they choose an informative action; and whether a representation is held
as revisable. Use Võ for the first distinction, existing Nelson/Narens for
monitoring/control, and Lutz for meta-awareness/dereification. Tatler supplies
task context. Van Dam constrains measurement language. These sources do not
jointly validate a single latent metacognitive score.

Supplement context should retain the existing independent prediction and
model-recovery requirements. Add separate gaze-memory/monitoring probes, actual
action records, reports about framing assumptions, and checks of how participants
understand instructions and questionnaire items. Probe timing can itself change
the task; assess that reactivity. Treat the proposed mapping from these measures
to the appreciation–practice–realization thesis as a testable design deduction,
not a result of the cited studies.

## Retrieval limitations and evidence checks

Web-tool requests to all four PMC article URLs returned browser checks. Direct
HTTP retrieval acquired real full text for Võ and Van Dam, but HTTP 200 for
Tatler and Lutz was only a browser-check page. Those bodies are retained and are
not counted as reading evidence. Author-hosted PDFs supplied Tatler and Lutz.
The four Europe PMC XML requests returned HTTP 500; their raw XML/error bodies
and headers remain in place. The web tool also reported inaccessible Europe PMC
article pages (one explicit 403). No access limit was silently converted to a
full-text claim. The ordinary SuttaCentral pages were shells; the institution's
translation repository supplied the texts. `web-observations.json` records those
tool-level failures separately from captured HTTP responses.

The follow-up Borji/Itti task-decoding paper was screened at primary abstract
level but is not proposed as a fifth addition: its full text was not inspected
in this pass. No claim about its methods or empirical accuracy is integrated.

`validate_research.py` records the exact source snapshot, confirms the four DOI
identities against captured Crossref records and their absence from the baseline
catalog, checks the two verbatim quotations against freshly retrieved editions,

quotation and a DOI and requires rejection. `verification.json` captures those
binary observations and the artifact inventory. These checks establish dossier
traceability; they do not validate the manuscript's scientific conclusions or
