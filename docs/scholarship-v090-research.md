# Focused scholarship for the four concepts

Research date: 2026-10-16. Scope: three new sources and targeted reinspection of two existing sources. This dossier recommends integration; it does not change numerical results or establish human efficacy. The existing inventory contained 72 keys. The new keys below were checked against the committed inventory to avoid duplicate sources.

Use the same labels throughout: **A Observed absence; B Missing observation; C Counterfactual value; D Non-reification.** Realizing Emptiness belongs explicitly in D, as the project's philosophical and software connection. Neither a change of model nor a score measures realizing emptiness. The four distinctions are editorial categories used here, not a taxonomy validated by the cited papers.

## B: Seaman, Galati, Jackson and Carlin (2013)

**New key:** `seaman2013`. *What Is Meant by “Missing at Random”?* Statistical Science 28(2), 257–268. [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/13-STS415); [author-deposited full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.2812).

**Read:** arXiv v1 PDF, §§1–2 (definitions), §5 introduction and §§5.1–5.2 (ignorability; Theorems 1–2). This is an electronic reprint with its own pagination, so use section/theorem locators. Crossref publisher-deposited metadata and the PDF agree on title, authors, year and DOI.

**Contribution:** Missingness indicators and observed values belong to distinct parts of the model. Ignoring the observation mechanism requires specified assumptions and an inferential target. Their Bayesian sufficient conditions combine realised MAR with independent priors for data-model and missingness-model parameters.

**Limit:** This is a methodological clarification, not a validation of this project's missing-data channel. MAR alone must not become shorthand for zero information, and a missing symbol does not establish ignorability.

**Proposed integration:** “B distinguishes an unavailable outcome from an observed absence. A missingness event can itself carry information; whether its mechanism may be ignored depends on explicit assumptions and the inference being made.” Cite beside the missing-observation definition, complementing the existing `rubin1976` metadata-only record.

**Artifacts:** `seaman2013.pdf`, `seaman2013.txt`, `seaman2013-crossref.json`.

## C: Epstude and Roese (2008)

**New key:** `epstude2008`. *The Functional Theory of Counterfactual Thinking.* Personality and Social Psychology Review 12(2), 168–192. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868308316091); [author manuscript at PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2408534/).

**Read:** full manuscript HTML, the old-mechanisms discussion of contrast and causal inference, the new-mechanisms account of content-specific/content-neutral pathways, and the reflection/evaluation discussion under general implications. The curl response contains the manuscript body; the web-reader response for the same URL was a browser-check page and was not used as full text. Crossref and manuscript identity match.

**Contribution:** The authors' functional framework separates pathways through specific action information from pathways through affect, motivation or processing style. Considering a worse alternative can produce favorable contrast, but vividly inhabiting that alternative can instead assimilate affect toward it.

**Limit:** This theory/review does not make avoided harm, relief and gratitude interchangeable, nor does it validate the project's utility equation as a human affect measure.

**Proposed integration:** “C evaluates an outcome against an explicit alternative. Favorable comparison need not be gratitude: counterfactual processing can produce contrast or assimilation and can affect feeling and action through different routes.” This earns a distinct citation without adding the overlapping Roese 1997 or inaccessible Markman–McMullen 2003 reviews.

**Artifacts:** `epstude2008-attempt.html` (successful full text despite conservative filename), `.txt`, `epstude2008-crossref.json`.

## D: Garfield and Thakchöe (2011 print edition)

**New key:** `garfieldthakchoe2011`. *Identifying the Object of Negation and the Status of Conventional Truth: Why the dGag Bya Matters So Much to Tibetan Mādhyamikas.* In The Cowherds, *Moonshadows: Conventional Truth in Buddhist Philosophy*, pp. 73–87. [Chapter DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199751426.003.0005); [author-hosted scanned chapter](https://jaygarfield.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/01/identifying_the_object_of_negation_and_t.pdf).

**Read:** targeted printed pp. 73–74, 76–85 and conclusion pp. 86–87. PDF pages 1–2 supply cover/title-page identity; PDF pages 3–10 contain the chapter. OCR was checked visually against the pp. 82–83 spread and the title page. **Date distinction:** the scanned title page says 2011; [Oxford's online book record](https://academic.oup.com/book/26720) and Crossref give 24 December 2010. Retain 2011 for the inspected print edition and record the online date separately. Do not silently change the date or use the different page range in secondary records.

**Contribution:** The chapter distinguishes negating intrinsic nature from negating conventional phenomena; p. 74 also rejects substituting emptiness as another intrinsic nature. It contrasts Tsongkhapa and Gorampa and does not adjudicate their disagreement.

**Limit:** This is philosophical analysis, not evidence that flexibility constitutes realization.

**Proposed integration:** “D withholds intrinsic status from representations while retaining their conditional use. This limited analogy with non-reification draws on an interpretation that preserves conventional efficacy; neither revision nor model switching establishes realizing emptiness.” Name the connection to Realizing Emptiness separately as the project's connection, not the chapter's finding.

**Artifacts:** `garfieldthakchoe2011.pdf`, `garfieldthakchoe2011-crossref.json`, `garfield2011-page-01.png` through `-10.png`, corresponding OCR files for pages 01–10. The initial `garfieldthakchoe2011.txt` contains only form feeds: it is preserved as an unsuccessful extraction, not substantive evidence.

## A: deeper access to existing Goh, Phillips and Firestone (2023)

**Existing key:** `goh2023`. *The perception of silence.* PNAS 120(29), e2301463120. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2301463120); [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10629541/).

**Read:** “General Discussion,” “Really Hearing Silence,” “Looking Ahead,” and the note comparing silence with holes. Crossref and PMC full-text identity match. The author-site PDF request returned HTTP 403 HTML; the independent PMC response contains the full article.

**Contribution and limit:** The experiments concern contrastive silences within structured auditory contexts. The authors explicitly leave absolute silence as a further question and caution that other modalities may lack a perfect analogy. This strengthens A without generalizing from silence to every absence or to an unobserved sensor value.

**Proposed integration:** “A concerns evidence-bearing absence within a functioning observation context, illustrated by perceptual representations of contrastive silence.” The functioning-channel phrase is the project's methodological distinction, not a claim that the paper tested sensor failure.

**Artifacts:** `goh2023-pmc.html`, `goh2023-pmc.txt`, `goh2023-crossref.json`; preserve failed `goh2023.pdf` plus headers/status.

## C: rechecked boundary in existing Koo et al. (2008)

**Existing key:** `koo2008`. [Author-hosted article](https://dtg.sites.fas.harvard.edu/KOO%20ALGOE%20WILSON%20GILBERT%20%282008%29.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0013316).

**Read:** pp. 1218–1220, especially Study 1 Procedure/Results and Study 2 Results. The title, four authors, year and DOI are printed in the freshly acquired article. Crossref retries returned HTTP 429 with empty bodies and supply no new metadata evidence.

**Contribution and limit:** Mental subtraction asks how an existing positive event might never have happened; it is not observation of an absent harm. Studies 1–2 report no significant condition difference on the direct event-gratitude item, despite the affect comparison. The previous inventory already records this boundary; retain it prominently rather than claiming novelty or calling positive affect gratitude.

**Proposed integration:** “Mental subtraction of an existing benefit motivates counterfactual appreciation, but does not identify detected safety, avoided harm and gratitude as one construct.”

**Artifacts:** `koo2008.pdf`, `koo2008.txt`; failed metadata responses and their headers/statuses.
