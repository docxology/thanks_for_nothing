## What the deeper readings change

**Constructs and obligations.** He and colleagues compared writing about a significant other, writing about health, and no writing. Gratitude and appreciation were highly correlated word ratings; the authors then analyzed gratitude rather than demonstrating discriminant validity. The indebtedness omnibus test was p = .077, while a selected significant-other/control comparison was p = .024. Writing time and length were not controlled. Thus the paper motivates measuring obligation separately, but supports neither a universal harm claim nor interchangeability of appreciation, gratitude and indebtedness. The model's benefactor-free valuation should remain a stipulated quantity, with Yu's abstract-level distinction between benefit and benefactor cost as additional context. [He et al.](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.824326); [Yu et al.](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2944-17.2018).

**Comparator content and attrition.** Cunha and colleagues randomized 1,337 people but analyzed 410 with post-baseline responses. Their neutral-event lists often contained positive material. Positive affect favored gratitude at post-test; their discussion reports no differences from neutral events in any assessed dimension at follow-up. Similar dropout across groups does not, by itself, recover outcomes for all randomized participants; that last point is our methodological inference. Kerry and colleagues' review further distinguishes neutral/negative from positive/active comparators, but acknowledges restrictive inclusion and a single-researcher search. Read these findings alongside the newer Choi abstract already in the catalog, not as proof that gratitude never helps. [Cunha et al.](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00584); [Kerry et al.](https://doi.org/10.2147/PRBM.S372432).

**Counterfactual subtraction is not an absence observation.** Koo and colleagues asked how an existing positive life event might never have occurred; later studies addressed forecasts and romantic relationships. The outcomes were affective states or relationship satisfaction; Studies 1–2 did not show significant differences on their direct event-gratitude item. This is different from recording an uneventful interval or establishing that an intervention prevented a harm. In this project, assigning a reference value while holding the posterior fixed is a designed computational separation. It is not a psychological finding recovered from Koo's experiments. Relief, appreciation and gratitude therefore need distinct empirical measures even where they share a contrastive evaluation. [Koo et al.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0013316).

**Attention needs more than a renamed parameter.** Bennett and colleagues induced affect after cue learning and examined reward generalization. Their model-based attention measure and gaze measure were not identical: reported gaze effects depended on probe type and on the denominator used for looking time. The article also describes eye-tracking quality exclusions. This provides a stronger reason to preserve separate simulated policy, choice and gaze endpoints than the abstract alone did. It does not establish that gratitude practice trains transferable precision, and it does not require this project's precision parameter to share the fitted attention weight's meaning. [Bennett et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011707).

**Emptiness does not mean indiscriminate absence.** MN 121 begins with specific absent occupants and a remaining community, then treats successive perceptual attainments while acknowledging residual disturbance. Its final passage describes even theme-less concentration as fashioned and impermanent. This textual progression discourages equating the absence category with total nonexistence or treating “nothingness” as the final object attained. Our connection to model non-reification is interpretive: retaining a model's conditions and limits can be a useful analogy, but neither label permutation nor reduced precision constitutes the text's contemplative accomplishment. The 1997 translation was revised on the host in 2013; the page points to a newer translation, which was not used here. [MN 121, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.121.than.html).

**Intervals require a target and a failure policy.** Morris and colleagues separate estimands from prediction/testing targets, warn that apparently competing methods can target different quantities, and require Monte Carlo uncertainty. White and colleagues advocate known-answer settings and inspection of failed estimates; an automatic backup changes the evaluated procedure, and deleting difficult replications can select the sample. Hall's abstract motivates considering percentile-t intervals, without supplying their assumptions here. The official `boot.ci` manual explicitly requires variance estimates for both the original statistic and resampled statistics, and flags extreme order-statistic endpoints as unreliable. [Morris et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8086); [White et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyad134); [Hall](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176350933); [boot.ci manual](https://www.stat.math.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/boot/html/boot.ci.html).

## Evidence-to-model mapping

The following are project design deductions, not additional results claimed by those papers.

| Literature constraint | Concrete model/reporting consequence | Test or empirical bridge still required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Benefit, benefactor attribution and obligation differ | Name the finite counterfactual value “absence appreciation”; do not call it a measured gratitude score | Separate gratitude, relief, indebtedness and perceived safety measures; benefactor manipulation |
| Neutral writing may contain positive material | Distinguish positive selection from replay dose and from the valence distribution in random recall | Matched exposure controls and an audit of selected observation content |
| Brief mental subtraction can change reported affect | Treat the implemented valuation-only operation as a deliberately narrower mechanism | Test whether human counterfactual reflection also changes recall, hazard beliefs or attention |
| Modeled attention and measured gaze can disagree | Preserve the exogenous precision comparison as a conditional model result | Held-out gaze/choice prediction and a separate training-to-transfer experiment |
| MN 121 distinguishes absence from residual presence | Keep detection, missingness and interpretive non-reification separate | Textual argument and empirical contemplative research cannot be replaced by a software invariant |
| Competing estimands may differ | Declare fixed world weights and the average of within-world paired contrasts before computing an interval | Confirm all interval methods target that same weighted contrast |
| Studentization needs variance per resample | Recompute the stratified variance within each resample; expose zero/nonfinite variance explicitly | Known-answer fixtures, independent arithmetic and empirical coverage for each fixture |
| Failures can change the estimand/performance denominator | Report unavailable intervals, valid replications and total replications; retain original intervals | Separate unconditional coverage from coverage conditional on availability |
| Monte Carlo rates are uncertain | Report uncertainty for coverage/rejection estimates separately from effect intervals | Adequate independent simulation repetitions and retained RNG identity |
| Gaussian controls are bounded | Add heavy-tail, skew and heterogeneous fixtures as distinct mathematical stress tests | Do not interpret their success as coverage on actual project contrasts or human trials |

For the proposed weighted mean, write the estimand as `theta(w) = sum_h w_h E[D_h]`, where `D_h` is the paired agent difference within world `h` and the weights sum to one. A bootstrap resamples paired agents within each world, not time points or world labels. Studentizing requires the original standard error and each resample's standard error for this same target. These equations describe the proposed analysis contract, not a completed implementation or a theorem extracted from Hall. Under fixed weights and independent agents across strata, the natural estimated variance is `sum_h w_h^2 s_h^2 / n_h`; any implemented correction needs its own numerical checks.

Changing the weights changes the target. Showing alternative world weights can reveal sensitivity to mixture composition, but it does not create new observations or demonstrate population generalizability. World-specific intervals should be labeled exploratory and their multiplicity distinguished from the original primary test family. A centered skew distribution supplies a mean-zero stress test; mean zero alone does not grant the symmetry required for an exact sign-flip interpretation. Rejection inflation there would expose a boundary, not a reason to retune the generator. Likewise, a low Brier score assesses predictive probability loss; it is not, by itself, proof of calibration. Calibration-specific conclusions need a matching diagnostic or decomposition.

## Candidate manuscript paragraphs

These paragraphs can be integrated by the manuscript owner. They make no new numerical claims about this repository's results.

**Constructs and counterfactuals.** Appreciation of an uneventful interval, relief that a feared event did not occur, and gratitude toward a benefactor need not denote the same process. Mental subtraction research concerns imagining that an existing positive event had not occurred, rather than observing an absence [@koo2008]. The finite appreciation quantity used here is therefore a proposed reference-dependent valuation. Its exact belief null is imposed by the implementation, and does not predict that human counterfactual reflection leaves beliefs unchanged.

**Intervention specificity.** The choice of comparison activity determines which explanation an intervention can address. In a randomized writing trial, the neutral-event activity itself often contained positive material [@cunha2019]. A review focused on life satisfaction likewise questions superiority over positive or psychologically active controls [@kerry2023]. Our matched replay comparison isolates a computational selection rule conditional on fixed exposures. It does not isolate human gratitude from expectancy, engagement, social meaning or demand characteristics.

**Statistical qualification.** We distinguish the target contrast, the interval procedure and the distribution used to evaluate that procedure [@morris2019]. The studentized bootstrap comparison requires a variance estimate for each resampled contrast as well as the original contrast [@bootci2026]. Undefined intervals and problematic replications must remain visible because conditioning on successful estimation can change the apparent performance [@white2024]. Additional distributional stress tests characterize mathematical operating conditions; they do not establish empirical power or clinical precision.

**Philosophical boundary.** In the named translation of MN 121, emptiness is assessed with respect to what is absent while acknowledging what remains; the discourse also treats refined concentration as conditioned [@thanissaro1997mn121]. We use this as a constraint on language, not as an identification of Buddhist practice with probabilistic inference. A permutation invariant or a likelihood update is a finite mathematical property, and neither measures realization.
