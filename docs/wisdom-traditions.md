# Ancient texts, wisdom traditions and the model's limits

The [comparative atlas data](../data/scholarship/traditions.json) now supplies the
manuscript's `world_traditions` PNG/SVG and the generated
[searchable source companion](../output/world_traditions.html). Both views use
the same admitted rows and catalog identities. Displayed cells are authored
interpretive summaries, not quotations or measurements. Expand a companion row
for its addressee, practice, named witness and non-equivalence; search includes
the expanded text. Selection follows the manuscript's questions and verified
sources, not a census or ranking of religions.

The [further primary-source dossier](research/traditions-comparison-20261020.md)
adds Japji Sahib, the Analects, Avot, Qur'an 31:12, Gītā 3.10–13 and Sanskrit MMK
context. It preserves the Legge XV.23 / Eno 15.24 numbering difference, Kulp's
word **lot**, the theological participants in hukam and yajña, and the difference
between gratitude's addressee and beneficiary. MMK prose is explicitly the
manuscript authors' paraphrase of the inspected Sanskrit, not a certified English
quotation. The Confucian row deliberately retains an ethical comparison without
an explicit gratitude instruction. A passage's silence is not a tradition's absence.

The pipeline binds row/catalog digests in `output/data/world_traditions.json`,
records that input's digest in the figure map and exactly replays the HTML.
Shared typography/layout constants remain in code; textual content lives in the
declarative data file. Stale rows, missing source identities, non-HTTPS links,
duplicate row identities and an altered companion fail admission or validation.

The manuscript uses named translations as textual arguments. It does not treat
scripture as experimental evidence or assume that different traditions mean the
same thing by emptiness. The [reading dossier](scholarship-v0110-research.md),
[scholarship catalog](../data/scholarship/sources.json) and
[bibliography](manuscript/references.bib) record editions, translators, access
scope, locators and interpretive ceilings.

The [expanded primary-text dossier](research/wisdom-traditions-20261020.md)
records the 2026-10-20 additions. The main manuscript now also compares Thich
Nhat Hanh's explicitly revised Heart Sutra rendering, Mukundananda's Bhagavadgītā,
NRSVUE thanksgiving, Kulp's Mishnah, the William Davidson Talmud edition,
Khattab's Qur'an translation and Rolt's Pseudo-Dionysius. Each passage has its
own object, addressee and purpose. In particular, Christian apophatic theology
is distinguished from biblical thanksgiving and from Madhyamaka's object of
negation. Unestablished edition years remain undated.

## Buddhist textual questions

*Saṃyutta Nikāya* (SN, Connected Discourses), *Majjhima Nikāya* (MN, Middle-length
Discourses) and *Aṅguttara Nikāya* (AN, Numerical Discourses) are collections of
early Buddhist scripture. Their identifiers locate texts; they are not model variables.

| Named translation | Interpretive question | Limit of the computational comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ṭhānissaro's Kaccayanagotta discourse, SN 12.15 | How are existence/non-existence extremes addressed through conditioned arising and cessation? | A probability update does not resolve that philosophical argument |
| Ṭhānissaro's Cula-suññata discourse, MN 121 | What is absent, what remains, and what is conditioned? | An informative no-event observation is not the discourse's complete practice |
| Ṭhānissaro's Suñña discourse, SN 35.85 | Empty of what: self or what pertains to self? | Non-self is not interchangeable with no event or missing data |
| Ṭhānissaro's Alagaddupama discourse, MN 22 | How can a useful support be relied upon and subsequently relinquished? | Retaining or changing a predictive family is not liberation |
| Ṭhānissaro's Kataññu discourses, AN 2.31–32 in the cited edition | How are gratitude, benefaction and ethical repayment connected? | Impersonal opportunity value is a constructive extension, not a translation of interpersonal gratitude |

The earlier [edition review](scholarship-v080-research.md) distinguishes translator
commentary from discourse, translation copyright from site revision, and an
inspected comparison translation from a claim of doctrinal consensus. The
[object-of-negation review](scholarship-v090-research.md) preserves the specific
later Madhyamaka interpretation separately. Siderits' historical discussion
motivates acknowledging transmitted texts and interpretive mediation.

## Functional absence and appreciation of capacities

Legge's inspected *Daodejing* digital edition, titled *The Tao Teh King*, offers
the wheel, vessel and room comparison in chapter 11. It helps articulate a
question about enabling conditions. Chan's account of competing interpretations
of *wu* and *wuwei* prevents a simple equation of Daoist nonbeing/nonaction with
Buddhist emptiness or computational inactivity.

Epictetus' *Discourses* I.6 considers perceptual and interpretive capacities in
an argument for divine providence. This sharpens the possible object of gratitude
while retaining a different metaphysical commitment from contingent inquiry.
The verified scanned Long edition, its targeted reading pages and the conflicting
TEI date remain disclosed in the dossier; the latter is not silently substituted.

## From comparison to a testable proposal

The project distinguishes appreciation of an avoided harm, value assigned
relative to an alternative, and appreciation of conditions that make an assumed
boundary examinable. Its finite mechanism separates value, chosen activity,
effective access, conditional retention/revision and independent prediction.
Those operations supply a bounded research program, not a realization metric.

A human study would need its own construct definition, translation-sensitive
practice instructions, suitable comparison activities, monitoring measures and
independent outcomes. A historical analogy cannot identify an opportunity-value
parameter or establish that gratitude causes insight. See
[evidence ceilings](evidence-ceilings.md), [the practice guide](emptiness-practice.md)
and [the manuscript discussion](manuscript/manuscript.md#sec-discussion).
