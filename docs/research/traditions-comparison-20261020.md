# Receiving benefits, relinquishing claims, and responding: comparative dossier

Verified live 2026-10-20. Read `CONTEXT.md`, manuscript section `sec-scripture`,
`docs/research/wisdom-traditions-20261020.md`, and the relevant existing bibliography
entries. This is the sole owned file; manuscript, bibliography, source catalog,
code, generated figures, and other workers' edits are outside this assignment.

## Argument and selection

The addition should ask **what response follows recognition of dependence or
benefit, and whether that response requires a new acquisition**. Five passages
give different answers: humility before divine order; restraint toward another;
rejoicing in one's existing lot; gratitude whose benefit accrues to the grateful;
and offerings in response to received gifts. This is a comparative question we
bring to the texts, not a shared ancient doctrine of gratitude for nothingness.
The Confucian passage is deliberately an ethical comparator: its inclusion tests
whether a comparison can retain a relevant difference without inventing an
emptiness teaching. MMK supplies a separate check on philosophical terminology.

Throughout, **T** denotes textual statement in the named edition, **I** our
interpretive comparison, and **M** a proposed modeled analogy. An I or M claim
must never acquire the authority of T by sharing its row or citation.

## Five defensible additions

### 1. Japji Sahib: hukam and speech without ego

**Edition/location.** Guru Nanak, *Japji Sahib*, in *Sri Guru Granth Sahib*,
**ang 1, pauri 2, final line**, following the declaration that everyone is within
hukam. Dr. Sant Singh Khalsa's English translation, SriGranth.org, undated web
edition. The page's selected translation identifies him; the site's
[acknowledgements](https://www.srigranth.org/guru_granth_sahib.html) independently
credit his translation as the default. Do not assign a print year to this display.
[Primary passage](https://www.srigranth.org/servlet/gurbani.gurbani?Action=Page&Param=1).

**Verified excerpt, 12 words:**

> O Nanak! One who understands His Command, does not speak in ego.

**T.** Pauri 2 places creation, status, pain, pleasure and grace under divine
Command; its conclusion links understanding that Command with the manner of
speaking. Pauri 1's closing instruction to walk in divine Will supplies immediate
practical context. The opening invocation makes the Creator and Guru's grace
explicit. **I.** This qualifies self-assertion rather than treating vacant space
as a good. **Limit.** *Hukam* and *haumai* are not respectively natural law and a
Bayesian self-model. The translated line does not claim empirical ego elimination,
and pauri 2 is not an explicit instruction to feel gratitude. Do not infer
fatalistic inactivity or Buddhist emptiness from it.

**Suggested manuscript use:** Guru Nanak's *Japji Sahib* connects understanding
divine hukam with speaking without ego (ang 1, pauri 2; Sant Singh Khalsa
translation). This offers a distinct theological comparison for relinquishing
self-assertion while remaining responsive to an order one does not author.

### 2. Analects: reciprocity as an ethical boundary

**Edition/location.** *Analects*, requested **15.24**, the exchange in which Zigong
asks for a lifelong rule and the Master answers with reciprocity (*shu*).
James Legge's *The Analects of Confucius (from the Chinese Classics)*,
[Project Gutenberg ebook 3330](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3330/pg3330-images.html),
digital release **2002-07-01**, updated 2021-01-08. **This inspected edition labels
the passage Book XV, chapter XXIII, not XXIV.** Robert Eno's
[2015 teaching translation, version 2.21](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/dspace/bitstreams/d1f62d31-9082-41d0-999f-4c3efa0c8857/download),
printed p. 85 / PDF page 100, labels the corresponding exchange **15.24**;
[institutional edition record](https://hdl.handle.net/2022/23420). Preserve this
edition-specific cross-reference rather than silently correcting Legge.

**Verified Legge excerpt, 13 words:**

> What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to others.

**T.** The answer specifies restraint in treatment of others as a rule of lifelong
practice. **I.** Relinquishing an imposition can have ethical significance without
supplying an object of gratitude. **Limit.** Reciprocity here is not a requirement
that another person first repay a benefit; its negative formulation constrains
one's own conduct. Neither this exchange nor its word for reciprocity establishes
an emptiness doctrine, gratitude practice, or philosophical identity with
non-reification. Do not turn this into a claim that Confucianism is simply secular.

**Suggested manuscript use:** The *Analects* offers an ethical comparator in its
injunction against doing to others what one would reject for oneself (15.24;
Legge XV.23). Here the relevant relinquishment is an imposition on another, not
an assertion that things lack intrinsic nature or a prescription of gratitude.

### 3. Mishnah Avot: rejoicing without requiring additional possessions

**Edition/location.** *Mishnah Avot* / Sefaria title *Pirkei Avot*, **4:1**, Ben
Zoma's third question, on wealth. **Mishnah Yomit by Dr. Joshua Kulp**, undated;
Sefaria metadata credits this named edition and records CC-BY. The reader did
not expose the passage to the web extractor; the
[version-specific first-party API](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Pirkei_Avot.4.1?context=0&commentary=0&ven=Mishnah_Yomit_by_Dr._Joshua_Kulp)
returned both text and metadata.
[Version-specific reader](https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.4.1?lang=bi&ven=Mishnah%20Yomit%20by%20Dr.%20Joshua%20Kulp).

**Verified excerpt, 9 words:**

> Who is rich? He who rejoices in his lot

**T.** The saying accompanies its answer with Psalm 128:2 about enjoying the fruit
of one's labor, and interprets happiness and good across this world and the world
to come. The wider mishnah also redescribes wisdom, might and honor through
learning, self-command and honoring others. **I.** Existing circumstances can be
valued without a new acquisition becoming the criterion of wealth. **Limit.**
Kulp says **lot**, not **portion**: the latter is a useful paraphrase, not his
verified quotation. Rejoicing is not automatically gratitude to a benefactor;
the selected clause specifies no addressee. It neither praises material
deprivation nor establishes an instruction to tolerate injustice.

**Suggested manuscript use:** Ben Zoma's account of wealth as rejoicing in one's
lot (*Avot* 4:1, Kulp translation) makes appreciation intelligible without a new
possession. Its scriptural and ethical setting distinguishes contentment with
what is available from a claim that nothing exists or that deprivation is good.

### 4. Qur'an: gratitude's beneficiary and God's self-sufficiency

**Edition/location.** **Sūrat Luqmān 31:12**, Dr. Mustafa Khattab,
*The Clear Quran*, credited by [Quran.com](https://quran.com/31/12), undated web
display; print year not established. Read the translated verse separately from
the site's tafsir. The immediate continuation, 31:13, presents Luqmān advising
his son against associating others with Allah.

**Verified excerpt, 11 words:**

> for whoever is grateful, it is only for their own good.

**T.** The verse introduces divinely bestowed wisdom, directs gratitude to Allah,
locates its good in the grateful person and affirms Allah's self-sufficiency and
praiseworthiness despite ingratitude. **I.** The beneficiary of gratitude and its
addressee need not occupy symmetrical positions of need. **Relation to existing
14:7.** This complements the already cited promise of increase by making the
beneficiary explicit; **14:8 already affirms divine self-sufficiency**, so do not
claim 31:12 first supplies that qualification.
[Verified 14:7](https://quran.com/en/ibrahim/7);
[verified 14:8](https://quran.com/en/ibrahim/8).
**Limit.** The good is not operationalized as money, accuracy or mental health.
Divine independence is not impersonal absence, and the verse does not remove
obligation or theological accountability.

**Suggested manuscript use:** Qur'an 31:12 directs gratitude to Allah while
locating its benefit in the grateful person and affirming divine self-sufficiency
(Khattab translation). Alongside 14:7–8, it distinguishes a theological account
of gratitude's beneficiary from a transaction in which God needs repayment or a
measured law of psychological gain.

### 5. Bhagavadgītā: received gifts and obligations of offering

**Edition/location.** **3.10–13**, Swami Mukundananda, *Bhagavad Gita: The Song of
God*, undated online translation and commentary. Read the separately headed
Translation for each verse:
[3.10](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/3/verse/10/),
[3.11](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/3/verse/11/),
[3.12](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/3/verse/12/),
[3.13](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/3/verse/13/).

**Verified excerpt from 3.12, 8 words:**

> without making offerings in return, are verily thieves.

**T.** In this rendering, 3.10 names Brahma's creation of humankind with duties;
3.11 describes sacrifices and cooperation between humans and celestial gods;
3.12 condemns enjoying gifts without return offerings; 3.13 contrasts eating
food first offered in sacrifice with cooking for one's own enjoyment. **I.**
Recognition of benefits can entail a practice of giving, not merely a favorable
feeling about them. This adds enacted obligation to the existing 2.47 discussion
of action without entitlement to fruits. **Limit.** Preserve *yajña* and the
divine participants: this is not generic ecological interdependence, Bayesian
exchange, or *shu*. The modern commentary's broader natural-system analogies
are not wording of the ancient verses, and the theological promises are not
experimental predictions.

**Suggested manuscript use:** The *Bhagavadgītā* 3.10–13 places received benefits
within a sacrificial relation between humans and divine participants, censuring
consumption without return offerings (Mukundananda translation). Compared with
2.47, this brings into view the difference between relinquishing entitlement to
results and undertaking a responsive practice.

## MMK: an attributed paraphrase is defensible; a borrowed translation is not

**Primary witness.** Nāgārjuna, *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā*,
[GRETIL / SUB Göttingen Sanskrit e-text](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_nAgArjuna-mUlamadhyamakakArikA.htm),
data entry Douglas Bachman, revision Richard Mahoney; TEI conversion dated
**2020-07-31**. The source field is blank: this is not an identified critical
print edition. The live text explicitly labels **MMK_24.8, MMK_24.9,
MMK_24.10 and MMK_24.18**. No English translator is credited by this witness.

**Verified Sanskrit 24.18, 11 words, separator retained:**

> yaḥ pratītyasamutpādaḥ śūnyatāṃ tāṃ pracakṣmahe / sā prajñaptir upādāya pratipat saiva madhyamā

**T, in our descriptive paraphrase.** Verse 24.18 connects dependent arising,
emptiness, designation in dependence, and the middle way. Verses 24.8–9 distinguish
conventional and ultimate truth; 24.10 makes reliance on ordinary convention
necessary for teaching the ultimate, and reaching the ultimate necessary for
attaining nirvāṇa. Resolving the feminine pronouns in 24.18 and specifying what
dependent designation entails involve interpretation. **I.** These connections
oppose treating emptiness as a substance called Nothing or discarding all
conventional distinctions. **M.** Conditional retention of a predictive family
is only a finite analogy; conventional truth is not defined by held-out accuracy.

**Publication-ready use, explicitly an authorial paraphrase, not a quotation:**
Nāgārjuna links dependent arising, emptiness and dependent designation in his
account of the middle way (*MMK* 24.18); his discussion of the two truths makes
conventional discourse indispensable to teaching the ultimate (24.8–10).
This motivates attention to the status of distinctions without identifying their
conditional computational usefulness with Buddhist realization.

Attribute that prose to the manuscript authors' reading of the named Sanskrit
witness; do not label it Garfield's English or put it in quotation marks. Retain
`garfieldthakchoe2011` for the existing secondary account of the object of
negation and differing interpretations of conventional truth. Its
[author-hosted scan](https://jaygarfield.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/01/identifying_the_object_of_negation_and_t.pdf)
returned image-only pages to text extraction here; no new quotation is certified
from that scan. `garfield1995` remains a bibliographic lead, not a freshly verified
English root-text quotation. A fuller English translation claim would require
checking that named translation and edition.

Use a matrix headed **Selected textual witnesses and distinct responses**.
Rows name passages and editions, not whole civilizations. Cells below are
interpretive summaries (I), anchored by the textual statements (T) above; they
are not verbatim translations or measurements. No row represents a latent wisdom
score, historical influence, or a progression toward the project's model.

| Textual witness | What is absent or relinquished | Appreciation / addressee | Ensuing practice | Non-equivalence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Japji, ang 1, pauri 2; Khalsa | Egoic speech in the concluding line; no literal absent object specified | Gratitude not explicit; divine hukam supplies the referent | Understand hukam; walking in divine Will in pauri 1 | Neither a self-model deletion nor Buddhist emptiness |
| Analects 15.24 / Legge XV.23 | Imposing unwanted treatment on another | No gratitude addressee specified; another person's treatment is at issue | Lifelong restraint through reciprocity | Ethical comparator; neither sacrificial repayment nor emptiness doctrine |
| Avot 4:1; Kulp | New acquisition need not define wealth (I); the text describes rejoicing in one's lot | Existing lot; no addressee in the selected clause | Rejoicing, with labor and scriptural context retained | Contentment is not automatically benefactor-directed gratitude |
| Qur'an 31:12; Khattab | No absence praised; Allah's need for human gratitude is denied | Allah; the grateful person benefits | Gratitude within divine instruction | Divine self-sufficiency is not missingness or an empirical utility function |
| Gītā 3.10–13; Mukundananda | Enjoyment without return offerings is censured | Received gifts; divine participants remain explicit | Sacrifice and eating what has first been offered | Theological reciprocity is not secular exchange or nonattachment alone |
| MMK 24.18 with 24.8–10; Sanskrit GRETIL | No empty object celebrated; rejection of reifying emptiness is I | Gratitude/addressee not specified | Discriminate the two truths; rely on convention in teaching | Conventional truth is not predictive accuracy; no computational realization criterion |

Keep any **M** connection in a separate caption sentence: *Our model asks whether
valuing access leads to costly examination while evidence independently governs
retention or revision; these texts neither implement nor validate that mechanism.*
The model does not implement interpersonal reciprocity, ritual sacrifice, divine
command or the Mishnah's account of wealth. An empty cell should read
*not specified in this passage*, never imply a tradition lacks the concept.

## Verification and limits

- Six excerpts were checked against successful live HTTPS responses using Bun
  1.4.3. Checks decoded HTML entities, removed markup, and normalized whitespace;
  they preserved words, punctuation and diacritics. Legge was restricted to Book
  XV; the Gītā excerpt to Translation before Commentary. Avot additionally
  required the exact API reference and version title. All six matched.
- The same checks rejected six deliberately mutated excerpts: Sikh egoic speech
  was affirmed instead of denied; Legge's restraint became an instruction to act;
  Avot's rejoicing became despair; Qur'anic grateful became ungrateful; Gītā's
  absence of offerings became their performance; MMK's emptiness received a
  negating prefix. These are negative controls, never alternative quotations.
  Quote lengths were respectively **12, 13, 9, 11, 8 and 11 words**, excluding
  the Sanskrit verse separator. A failed match would fail the command.
- All four Gītā verse translations were read; all four MMK stanza labels and
  their Sanskrit context were checked live. Eno's page independently resolves
  the Analects numbering. Chinese Text Project returned HTTP 403, so no wording
  or attribution depends on reading that blocked page. Sefaria's reader supplied
  interface text; its first-party version-specific API supplied the passage.
- No manuscript rendering or scientific pipeline was run: this task produces
  research suggestions, not an integrated manuscript or finished figure. Exact
  matching verifies custody of wording, not adequacy of interpretation. This is
  selective comparison, not a philological collation, comprehensive traditions
  survey, historical influence argument or human-efficacy finding.
- GitVault was confirmed mounted before writing. Network bodies were processed
  in memory; none was saved in the repository. Task subprocess `TMPDIR` was on
  GitVault; no Python environment was invoked. No commits or external writes
  were made, and no further agents were delegated.
