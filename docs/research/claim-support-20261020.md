## 1. Cronbach and Meehl: interpreting a measure as a construct

**Bibliography:** Lee J. Cronbach and Paul E. Meehl. 1955. “Construct validity in psychological tests.” *Psychological Bulletin* **52**(4): 281–302. DOI: [10.1037/h0040957](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0040957). Names, issue, pages and July date match [publisher-deposited Crossref metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1037/h0040957).

**Access and inspection:** Targeted full text in York University's [primary-article transcription](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Cronbach/construct.htm), retaining original page markers: “Four Types of Validation” (pp. 281–283), “Experimentation to Investigate Construct Validity,” especially mathematical analysis (pp. 287–290), “The Nomological Net” (pp. 290–291), and “Implications of Negative Evidence” (pp. 294–296). A [Minnesota-hosted reprint](https://users.cla.umn.edu/~nwaller/prelim/cronmeehl.pdf) downloaded, but the browsing reader rejected its size; it is not the inspected pagination witness.

**Narrow support:** A proposed interpretation generates testable relations; adverse results can implicate the measure, theory or design. A changed interpretation requires fresh validation. These points support separating an implemented operation from its psychological interpretation. [Primary text, named sections](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Cronbach/construct.htm).

**Suggested insertion:** Discussion, `sec-main-identification`, after the paragraph proposing separate human measurements:

> Interpreting inquiry choices as gratitude requires testable relations to other observations, rather than identification by the task label alone. Changes to that interpretation would require fresh validation evidence [@cronbachmeehl1955].

**Avoid:** Claiming that predictive improvement, questionnaire agreement, internal consistency or one significant manipulation check validates gratitude or realization. This historical methodological argument supplies no gratitude-specific validation.

## 2. Palminteri, Wyart and Koechlin: relative success versus behavioral adequacy

**Bibliography:** Stefano Palminteri, Valentin Wyart and Etienne Koechlin. 2017. “The Importance of Falsification in Computational Cognitive Modeling.” *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* **21**(6): 425–433. DOI: [10.1016/j.tics.2017.03.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2017.03.011). Verified against [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/j.tics.2017.03.011) and the author's [ENS publication record](https://www.cognition.ens.fr/en/publication/657/stefano-palminteri). Issue June; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28476348/) records online publication on 2 May.

**Access and inspection:** Publisher full-text requests returned 403. Inspected the primary article itself in a [non-author researcher-hosted journal PDF](https://rebekahmanweiler.github.io/resources/documents/Palminteri2017.pdf): opening comparison/falsification argument and glossary (pp. 425–426), “Model Selection in Cognitive Sciences: Proposed Guidelines,” steps i–vi (pp. 430–432), and Box 2 (pp. 430–431). Title, authors, DOI and pagination match first-party metadata. This is targeted full-text access, not publisher-hosted access.

**Narrow support:** Relative ranking can favor a model whose simulations fail to reproduce the behavioral effect at issue; the authors recommend task discrimination, recovery and simulation-based assessment. [Primary article, guidelines and Box 2](https://rebekahmanweiler.github.io/resources/documents/Palminteri2017.pdf).

**Suggested insertion:** Discussion, `sec-main-identification`, after “A menu member winning a comparison still need not explain the behavior adequately”:

> Relative model comparison should be accompanied by checks that simulations reproduce the behavioral pattern the model is intended to explain [@palminteri2017].

**Avoid:** Treating replay, model recovery or a winning Bayes factor as proof of correctness. Their term “predictive performance” can concern fitted likelihood; it is not synonymous with independent held-out performance. Use Wilson and Collins for the manuscript's separate parameter/model-recovery distinction.

## 3. Gelman and Shalizi: checking and revising the candidate family

**Bibliography:** Andrew Gelman and Cosma Rohilla Shalizi. 2013. “Philosophy and the practice of Bayesian statistics.” *British Journal of Mathematical and Statistical Psychology* **66**(1): 8–38. DOI: [10.1111/j.2044-8317.2011.02037.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-8317.2011.02037.x). [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/j.2044-8317.2011.02037.x) distinguishes online publication, 24 February 2012, from the February 2013 issue; cite 2013 consistently.

**Access and inspection:** Targeted full text in the [author-hosted published PDF](https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/research/published/philosophy.pdf), §2 “The data-analysis cycle” (pp. 11–12) and §4 “Model checking,” through §4.2 (pp. 17–21), including posterior predictive checks, test severity and model inadequacy. Full PDF available; not an exhaustive reading of every philosophical argument or cited theorem.

**Narrow support:** The authors distinguish posterior calculation from checking implications against observations, and use discrepancies to motivate model changes. Passing a check depends on its ability to reveal relevant failure. [Primary article, §§2, 4](https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/research/published/philosophy.pdf).

**Suggested insertion:** Discussion, `sec-main-identification`, immediately after the opening paragraph on authored assumptions:

> Updating within a specified candidate family does not replace checking that family's implications against observations; discrepancies may instead motivate changing the family itself [@gelmanshalizi2013].

**Avoid:** Presenting this philosophical position as consensus, Bayesian updating as inherently corrective, or the finite project's software controls as completed empirical posterior predictive checks. Neither model revision nor this statistical philosophy establishes Buddhist non-reification or realization.

## 4. Lakens: evidence of equivalence requires a specified margin

**Bibliography:** Daniël Lakens. 2017. “Equivalence Tests: A Practical Primer for t Tests, Correlations, and Meta-Analyses.” *Social Psychological and Personality Science* **8**(4): 355–362. DOI: [10.1177/1948550617697177](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177). [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/1948550617697177) stores the subtitle separately; the [publisher article](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1948550617697177) displays the complete title and online date, 5 May 2017.

**Access and inspection:** Targeted publisher HTML full text via the browsing reader: opening argument; “Testing for Equivalence,” including Figure 1; “Setting Equivalence Bounds”; and “Discussion.” Direct HTTP downloads from both SAGE and Eindhoven returned 403; saved `.html`/`.pdf` responses from those requests are challenge pages, not article receipts.

**Narrow support:** Nonsignificance does not establish equivalence. TOST tests effects outside prespecified bounds; its usual two-sided 90% interval criterion at one-sided alpha .05 requires the interval to lie wholly inside those bounds. Conclusions concern the specified operationalization. [Primary article, named sections](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1948550617697177).

**Suggested insertion:** Results, `sec-main-gaze`, after the sentence describing a bounded lack of evidence; alternatively use the prospective-design supplement, not both:

> A claim of practical equivalence would require prespecified meaningful bounds and an analysis designed to exclude effects outside them; an interval containing zero does not supply that conclusion [@lakens2017].

**Avoid:** Retrofitting margins to current results; calling a Bayes factor, a nonsignificant difference or overlapping intervals a TOST result; exporting the simple-test rule to joint bootstrap rectangles without justification. Algebraically proved identities remain distinct from statistical equivalence.

## 5. Yarkoni: align the generalization target with the design

**Bibliography:** Tal Yarkoni. 2022. “The generalizability crisis.” *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* **45**, e1. DOI: [10.1017/S0140525X20001685](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X20001685). [Publisher record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/abs/generalizability-crisis/AD386115BA539A759ACB3093760F4824) and [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1017/S0140525X20001685) distinguish online publication, 21 December 2020, from issue year 2022. Use e1 rather than assigning the whole commentary treatment to the target article.

**Access and inspection:** Cambridge supplied abstract/metadata only and PMC presented a browser challenge. Targeted full text instead in the [UC San Diego-hosted published treatment](https://lillab.ucsd.edu/assets/pdf/ManyBabies_BBS_commentary.pdf): target article §1 (pp. 1–2), §§2–3.2 (pp. 2–5), and §6.3.1 “Draw more conservative inferences” (p. 10). The 78-page PDF also contains commentaries and the response; those were not reviewed or attributed to Yarkoni's target article.

**Narrow support:** Statistical specifications and verbal claims need aligned universes of observations; generalization can concern stimuli, tasks and settings as well as participants. [Primary article, §§1–3.2, 6.3.1](https://lillab.ucsd.edu/assets/pdf/ManyBabies_BBS_commentary.pdf).

**Suggested insertion:** Discussion, `sec-main-identification`, following the human-study proposal:

> Prospective generalization claims should name the participants, stimuli, instructions and contexts they concern, with designs and analyses that address variation over those targets [@yarkoni2022].

**Avoid:** Assuming more simulated agents sample human populations; treating random effects as an automatic repair for construct validity or unrepresentative designs; interpreting this argument as evidence that the proposed human effect exists.
