# Emptiness, scripture and the object of appreciation: source dossier

Verified online 2026-10-20. Scope: research for the section of that name in
`docs/manuscript/manuscript.md`; suggestions only, with no manuscript or bibliography
edits. Read the section, the relevant entries in `references.bib`, and
`docs/wisdom-traditions.md`. Existing keys `thanissaro1997mn121` and
`laozilegge2008` can be reused. `garfieldthakchoe2011` is a secondary philosophical
discussion, not a primary translation of Nāgārjuna.

## Selection and use

Seven core primary-text selections follow. Three short companion checks cover
Madhyamaka, Christian apophasis, and the requested Talmudic counterpart: ten
distinct works in total, rather than silently treating those as the seven core
sources. Each work has one verified quotation of at most 25 words. Quotations
are excerpts, not summaries; line wrapping is normalized. Brackets and the
ellipsis in the Jewish excerpts belong to the editions. Interpretations below
are this dossier's bounded readings, not claims of doctrinal consensus.

The strongest expansion asks what is absent, what remains useful, what is
relinquished, and to whom appreciation is directed. The chosen texts do not all
teach gratitude, and they do not share a single concept of nothingness.

## Seven core selections

### 1. Early Buddhist emptiness: MN 121

- **Text/edition:** *Cula-suññata Sutta: The Lesser Discourse on Emptiness*,
  translated from Pali by Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu, copyright 1997; Access to Insight,
  BCBS edition, revised 2013-11-30. The host states that the transcription came
  from a file supplied by the translator.
- **Exact location:** MN 121, PTS M iii 104; first wilderness-perception sequence,
  immediately before the heading *The Perception of Earth*.
- **Verified quotation, 9 words:**

  > Whatever remains, he discerns as present: 'There is this.'

- **Interpretation:** Absence is specified relative to a perceptual dwelling, with
  a remainder acknowledged. This supports asking what an emptiness claim negates.
  The later Release section also examines the conditioned character of refined
  concentration; this is not simply an instruction to appreciate vacant space.
- **Caveat:** Pali *suññatā* here is not interchangeable with missing data,
  universal nonexistence, or every later Mahāyāna interpretation. The quotation
  matches the manuscript's named historical translation; the host warns of newer
  versions. [Primary translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.121.than.html).

### 2. Heart Sutra: a clearly identified contemporary rendering

- **Text/edition:** *The Insight that Brings Us to the Other Shore*, Thich Nhat
  Hanh's September 2014 English Heart Sutra translation, published by his Plum
  Village community; the page says it follows his new Vietnamese rendering.
- **Exact location:** Sutra text, first address to Sariputra, opening two lines
  about Body and Emptiness, after the five-skandhas opening.
- **Verified quotation, 11 words:**

  > this Body itself is Emptiness and Emptiness itself is this Body.

- **Interpretation:** The paired formulation resists placing emptiness in a
  separate realm that abolishes embodied phenomena. It can introduce inquiry
  into how something is held to exist, rather than appreciation of nonexistence.
- **Caveat:** This is a deliberately interpretive revision. In his accompanying
  explanation, Hanh explicitly identifies additions and changes intended to
  prevent nihilistic readings. Do not silently replace his Body with another
  edition's form, or present his explanatory changes as an unmodified ancient
  Sanskrit text. His historical claims in the explanation were not independently
  verified here. [Translator's community publication and explanation](https://plumvillage.org/about/thich-nhat-hanh/letters/thich-nhat-hanh-new-heart-sutra-translation).

### 3. Daoist useful absence: Daodejing 11

- **Text/edition:** Laozi, *The Tao Teh King, or the Tao and its Characteristics*,
  James Legge translation; Project Gutenberg ebook 216, HTML release 2008-07-12,
  updated 2013-02-04. These dates identify the digital edition.
- **Exact location:** Part I, chapter 11, second sentence, vessel example.
- **Verified quotation, 16 words:**

  > Clay is fashioned into vessels; but it is on their empty hollowness, that their use depends.

- **Interpretation:** Wheel, vessel and room examples make an absence useful
  through its relationship to an arrangement. This is a particularly direct
  comparison for appreciating enabling conditions.
- **Caveat:** The chapter concerns use, not an explicit gratitude practice.
  Legge's explanatory parentheses elsewhere in the chapter are translation
  choices. Daoist *wu* should not be equated with Buddhist *śūnyatā*, a sensor's
  no-event outcome, or the absence of a recorded observation.
  [Verified digital primary edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/216/216-h/216-h.htm).

### 4. Hindu nonattachment: Bhagavadgītā 2.47

- **Text/edition:** Swami Mukundananda's online *Bhagavad Gita: The Song of God*,
  translation and commentary. Sanskrit, transliteration, word glosses, translation
  and commentary are separately displayed; no print-publication year is inferred.
- **Exact location:** Chapter 2, verse 47, first sentence under Translation.
- **Verified quotation, 20 words:**

  > You have a right to perform your prescribed duties, but you are not entitled to the fruits of your actions.

- **Interpretation:** The object relinquished is entitlement to action's fruits,
  not action itself. The verse's final clause rejects attachment to inaction.
  This sharpens the difference between valuing inquiry and demanding a favorable
  result from it.
- **Caveat:** This verse does not itself define gratitude or Buddhist emptiness.
  Rendering *adhikāra* as right is translation-sensitive; the surrounding
  duty-oriented and theological setting should remain visible. The site's
  commentary makes practical-benefit claims; this dossier does not adopt them as
  empirical evidence. [Primary translation, verse and commentary](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/2/verse/47/).

### 5. Christian thanksgiving: 1 Thessalonians 5:18

- **Text/edition:** *New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition* (NRSVUE),
  copyright 2021, National Council of Churches of Christ in the United States
  of America; authorized text displayed by Bible Gateway, operated by
  HarperCollins Christian Publishing. This is a named primary-text edition,
  rather than a quotation from a devotional explanation.
- **Exact location:** 1 Thessalonians 5:18, entire verse.
- **Verified quotation, 17 words:**

  > give thanks in all circumstances, for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you.

- **Interpretation:** Thanksgiving is situated in circumstances and a Christic
  relation to God. Appreciation need not wait for an explicitly favorable
  outcome.
- **Caveat:** In this wording, thanksgiving in circumstances does not by itself
  establish that every harmful event is a benefit. Nor does this verse teach
  apophatic theology; the companion below supplies a separate Christian
  theological source. [Authorized primary-text display](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Thessalonians%205%3A18&version=NRSVUE).

### 6. Islamic gratitude: Qur'an 14:7

- **Text/edition:** *The Clear Quran*, translation by Mustafa Khattab, as
  explicitly credited on Quran.com, a Quran Foundation text platform. The
  translation is distinguished from the page's Ibn Kathir tafsir material.
- **Exact location:** Sūrat Ibrāhīm, 14:7, divine proclamation within the verse.
- **Verified quotation, 10 words:**

  > If you are grateful, I will certainly give you more.

- **Interpretation:** Gratitude is addressed within a relation to the Lord,
  accompanied by a promise. Surrounding verses 14:6–8 frame Moses' reminder of
  [divine deliverance](https://quran.com/en/ibrahim/6) and God's independence from
  human gratitude.
- **Caveat:** The remainder of 14:7 warns against ingratitude; do not omit that
  asymmetry when describing its teaching. Arabic *shukr* and *kufr* have religious
  meanings that this English polarity only partly conveys. The English gift
  formulation is a translator's rendering, not a quantified causal law about
  wealth, optimism, or psychological efficacy.
  [Primary verse and credited translation](https://quran.com/en/ibrahim/7).

### 7. Jewish blessing: Mishnah Berakhot 9:5

- **Text/edition:** *Mishnah Yomit by Dr. Joshua Kulp*, English edition served by
  Sefaria; version metadata identifies Kulp, the Conservative Yeshiva source,
  and CC-BY licensing. Do not attribute this wording to Sefaria's default
  William Davidson edition.
- **Exact location:** Mishnah Berakhot 9:5, opening clause, before the citation
  of Deuteronomy 6:5.
- **Verified quotation, 17 words:**

  > One must bless [God] for the evil in the same way as one blesses for the good,

- **Interpretation:** Blessing is an obligation directed to God across divergent
  circumstances; it does not erase the distinction between evil and good.
- **Caveat:** The final comma is retained because this is an opening-clause
  excerpt. Blessing, praise and gratitude overlap without being interchangeable.
  The Talmudic discussion below explicitly distinguishes the formulas for good
  and bad tidings. [Version-specific reader](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Berakhot.9.5?lang=bi&ven=Mishnah%20Yomit%20by%20Dr.%20Joshua%20Kulp);
  [version-specific first-party text API used to verify wording and metadata](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Mishnah_Berakhot.9.5?context=0&commentary=0&ven=Mishnah_Yomit_by_Dr._Joshua_Kulp).

## Three companion checks

### A. Madhyamaka: Nāgārjuna, Mūlamadhyamakakārikā 24.18

**Edition and exact location:** GRETIL, SUB Göttingen, Sanskrit electronic text
entered by Douglas Bachman, revised by Richard Mahoney; HTML/TEI conversion
dated 2020-07-31. The stanza is explicitly marked `MMK_24.18`.

**Verified quotation, 11 Sanskrit words; verse separator retained:**

> yaḥ pratītyasamutpādaḥ śūnyatāṃ tāṃ pracakṣmahe / sā prajñaptir upādāya pratipat saiva madhyamā

**Interpretation:** The stanza connects dependent arising, emptiness, dependent
designation, and the middle way. Nearby 24.8–10 distinguish the two truths and
the role of conventional discourse. This supplies an argument for examining
intrinsic nature, not celebrating a substance called Nothing.

**Caveat:** This is verified Sanskrit, not an independently verified English
quotation. There is no translator to credit for the excerpt. The conversion's
underlying print-source field is blank; do not invent a critical edition.
Resolving the referents and rendering *upādāya prajñapti* requires interpretation.
Use the existing `garfieldthakchoe2011` for its expressly attributed discussion,
not as though it were the root text. [Institutional primary e-text](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_nAgArjuna-mUlamadhyamakakArikA.htm).

### B. Christian apophasis: Pseudo-Dionysius, Mystical Theology V

**Edition and exact location:** C. E. Rolt's English translation in *Dionysius
the Areopagite: On the Divine Names and the Mystical Theology*, CCEL online
transcription; *Mystical Theology*, chapter V, final sentence. Its title page
identifies a Kessinger Publishing Company edition, Montana, ISBN 0-922802-97-1.
No publication year is established by the inspected title page.

**Verified quotation, 14 words, excerpt from the final sentence:**

> It transcends all affirmation by being the perfect and unique Cause of all things

**Interpretation:** The same sentence also places the Cause beyond negation.
Chapter II joins the removal of predicates to praise. This gives a Christian
example of praise alongside a declared limit on conceptual predication.

**Caveat:** This is Christian theological writing, not a biblical verse or a
general negation of God. Its transcendent Cause differs from Madhyamaka's
argument. The electronic text interleaves Rolt's explanatory notes with the
translation; do not quote his notes as the ancient author's words.
[Primary translation, chapters I–V](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/rolt/dionysius.v.html);
[inspected edition title page](https://ccel.org/ccel/rolt/dionysius/dionysius.i.html).

### C. Jewish ordinary capacities: Babylonian Talmud, Berakhot 60b

**Edition and exact location:** William Davidson Edition – English, based on the
Koren Noé Talmud with commentary by Rabbi Adin Even-Israel Steinsaltz, Sefaria;
Berakhot 60b:5, blessing upon opening the eyes.

**Verified quotation, 7 words:**

> Blessed…Who gives sight to the blind.

**Interpretation:** A routine capacity becomes an occasion for blessing rather
than needing to be an exceptional acquisition. Separately, 60b:7 discusses
Mishnah 9:5: it preserves different formulas for good and bad tidings and
interprets the common requirement through reception of the news.

**Caveat:** The printed ellipsis abbreviates the blessing formula; it is not a
new omission introduced here. This annotated English edition expands the
source, so distinguish translated formula from editorial explanation. Do not
collapse Berakhot 60b's Gemara into the Mishnah, or infer a psychological effect
from either. [Version-specific primary passage](https://www.sefaria.org/Berakhot.60b.5?lang=bi&ven=William%20Davidson%20Edition%20-%20English);
[first-party text API used for verification](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Berakhot.60b.5?context=0&commentary=0&ven=William_Davidson_Edition_-_English);
[interpretive discussion, 60b:7](https://www.sefaria.org/Berakhot.60b.7?lang=bi&ven=William%20Davidson%20Edition%20-%20English).

## Integration guidance

1. Retain the current early Buddhist sequence and its distinction between
   textual interpretation and finite modeling. Add the Heart Sutra and
   Madhyamaka as separately attributed developments, not equivalent restatements
   of MN 121.
2. Use Daodejing 11 for usefulness through absence, and Gītā 2.47 for acting
   without entitlement to results. Neither passage explicitly establishes a
   gratitude-for-nothing practice.
3. Give thanksgiving and blessing their named divine addressees. The Christian,
   Islamic and Jewish passages illuminate the relation between circumstance,
   response and addressee; they do not all make absence the object of appreciation.
4. Keep apophatic predication separate from thanksgiving. The comparison is
   between praise and limits on conceptual description, not a claim that
   Pseudo-Dionysius and Nāgārjuna negate the same thing.
5. Label appreciation of impersonal conditions of inquiry as this project's
   constructive proposal. None of these texts validates the model's opportunity
   value, posterior update, dereification measure, or a gratitude-to-realization
   causal path. A comparison can generate a question without establishing
   historical influence, philosophical equivalence, or empirical efficacy.

## Verification limits and custody

- Network browsing verified the primary passages and displayed edition details.
  Sefaria's reader pages returned interface text without the passages to the web
  extractor; direct HTTPS GET requests to its version-specific text API supplied
  the wording and metadata. API bodies were processed in memory, not saved.
- The 84000 Heart Sutra page and attempted PDF URL returned a JavaScript shell to
  the extractor. No 84000 quotation is represented as verified. The selected
  Plum Village publication is explicit about its different translation status.
- USCCB's 1 Thessalonians page and Chinese Text Project returned HTTP 403. The
  dossier instead cites the verified authorized NRSVUE display and the existing
  Gutenberg Legge edition. An attempted Batchelor translation URL returned 404;
  no quotation or edition claim is based on that missing page.
- The Madhyamaka companion verifies the Sanskrit only; a publication-ready
  English quotation of MMK 24.18 still requires checking a named translation.
  The dossier does not invent one. The inspected CCEL title page supports the
  named Rolt/Kessinger electronic edition, not an assumed original-print date.
- This is selective primary-text reading, not a philological collation,
  systematic survey, proof of historical influence, or efficacy review. Ancient
  composition dates, doctrinal consensus and contemporary clinical claims were
  not established.
- All ten excerpts matched live source text in a final automated check after
  markup removal and whitespace normalization. A deliberately corrupted version
  of each excerpt was rejected by the same comparison. All remain below the
  25-word ceiling; the Sanskrit separator is punctuation, and the printed
  ellipsis separates two words in the Berakhot excerpt.
- GitVault was confirmed mounted before the write. No raw copyrighted source
  downloads, task cache files, remote writes or commits were made. The sole
  authored artifact is this dossier; other workers' changes were preserved.
