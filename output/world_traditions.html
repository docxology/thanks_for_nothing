Text, practice, sources & limits

Textual basis / appreciation. No gratitude addressee is specified in these selected passages. MN 121 distinguishes an absent disturbance from remaining presence; SN 35.85 specifies self and what pertains to self as the object of emptiness.

Practice / response. Discern the conditioned character of what remains; the later release sequence in MN 121 exceeds a simple absent-event judgment.

Our comparison. Thanks for Nothing is our question about appreciating an enabling absence; the texts constrain its object rather than naming this gratitude practice. Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu translations.

Interpretive limit. A functioning detector's no-event category neither exhausts these discourses nor measures liberation. Empty of self is not missing data.

MahāyānaHeart; MMK 24.18
Absence / relinquishmentEmptiness is not a separate Nothing.
Appreciation / responseRetain phenomena and conventional discourse.
Text, practice, sources & limits

Textual basis / appreciation. Gratitude is not specified here. Hanh's body/emptiness formulation is an explicitly revised 2014 translation. Our paraphrase of MMK 24.18 connects dependent arising, emptiness and dependent designation.

Practice / response. MMK 24.8–10 distinguishes two truths and makes convention necessary for teaching the ultimate. These passages and the Heart Sutra have different textual settings.

Our comparison. The comparison concerns non-reification and conditional use of distinctions. Neither a reduced candidate menu nor a new predictive family establishes realization.

Interpretive limit. The GRETIL witness is Sanskrit; its print-source field is blank. The MMK summary is our paraphrase, not a verified English quotation. Conventional truth is not defined by predictive accuracy.

DaoistDaodejing 11
Absence / relinquishmentUnfilled hollow within a formed vessel.
Appreciation / responseRecognize what an arrangement enables.
Text, practice, sources & limits

Textual basis / appreciation. The hollow is useful in relation to the vessel's form; wheel and room examples likewise concern configurations. No grateful addressee is specified.

Practice / response. Attend to the contribution of what is unfilled without treating all structure as dispensable. James Legge translation, chapter 11.

Our comparison. Thanks for Nothing can name appreciation of an enabling unfilled condition, provided its usefulness is specified relationally.

Interpretive limit. Daoist wu and wuwei are not simply Buddhist śūnyatā or inactivity. This passage is not an empirical gratitude intervention.

Hindu / Gītā2.47; 3.10–13
Absence / relinquishmentRelinquish entitlement to action's fruits.
Appreciation / responseAct; respond to gifts through offerings.
Text, practice, sources & limits

Textual basis / appreciation. Action does not entitle one to its fruits (2.47). Received gifts also carry sacrificial obligations involving human and divine participants (3.10–13). Mukundananda translation.

Practice / response. Prescribed action, rejection of attachment to inaction, sacrifice and food first offered: response is enacted rather than merely felt.

Our comparison. Relinquishing a demand for a preferred result can coexist with responsive action; gratitude for inquiry does not entitle an investigator to confirmation.

Interpretive limit. Yajña retains its theological participants. Neither nonattachment nor offering is generic Bayesian exchange, and the model implements no ritual sacrifice.

StoicEpictetus I.6
Absence / relinquishmentNo new possession is required here.
Appreciation / responseAttend to perception and understanding.
Text, practice, sources & limits

Textual basis / appreciation. Ordinary capacities for perception and understanding appear within Epictetus's argument for divine providence. George Long translation, 1887 scan, I.6, pp. 19–21.

Practice / response. Recognize and respond to capacities already available rather than requiring a fresh acquisition before appreciation is possible.

Our comparison. The capacity that makes inquiry possible can itself become an object of appreciation; its interpretation remains providential in this passage.

Interpretive limit. The providential argument is neither impersonal missingness nor an empirical claim that gratitude improves cognition. The digital transcription's conflicting date is disclosed in the dossier.

JewishAvot 4:1; Ber. 9:5
Absence / relinquishmentNew acquisition need not define wealth.
Appreciation / responseRejoice in one's lot; distinguish blessings.
Text, practice, sources & limits

Textual basis / appreciation. Ben Zoma's saying values one's existing lot (Avot 4:1, Kulp). Blessing God across good and evil (Berakhot 9:5, Kulp) addresses a separate liturgical question.

Practice / response. Avot invokes enjoyment of labor's fruit and its scriptural setting. Berakhot 60b:7 (William Davidson edition) preserves different blessing formulas for good and bad tidings.

Our comparison. Appreciation can concern what is already available while the evaluation of adverse circumstances remains discriminating. The two passages supply distinct arguments.

Interpretive limit. Contentment is not automatically benefactor-directed gratitude. Blessing across circumstances does not make good and bad equivalent or justify deprivation and injustice.

Christian1 Thess. 5:18
Absence / relinquishmentNo favorable outcome is a prerequisite.
Appreciation / responseGive thanks in circumstances, toward God.
Text, practice, sources & limits

Textual basis / appreciation. The NRSVUE instruction places thanksgiving within God's will in Christ Jesus. It supplies a theological addressee rather than a numerical positivity preference.

Practice / response. Give thanks in all circumstances, while distinguishing the instruction from the claim that every harmful event is a benefit.

Our comparison. Thanks for Nothing can ask whether thanksgiving must wait for a welcome result; this verse's theological relation is retained.

Interpretive limit. This sentence does not establish clinical efficacy, an apophatic doctrine, or an obligation to regard actual harm as good.

IslamicQur'an 14:7; 31:12
Absence / relinquishmentGod's need for repayment is denied.
Appreciation / responseThank Allah; the grateful person benefits.
Text, practice, sources & limits

Textual basis / appreciation. Khattab's translation locates gratitude's benefit in the grateful person (31:12) and affirms Allah's self-sufficiency; 14:7–8 pairs promised increase and accountability with divine independence.

Practice / response. Gratitude within divine instruction. Distinguish the beneficiary from the addressee without removing theological obligation.

Our comparison. The response need not supply something its addressee lacks. This differs from appreciation of an empty vessel or from Bayesian updating.

Interpretive limit. Divine self-sufficiency is not impersonal absence. The good is not operationalized as wealth, predictive accuracy, mental health or a symmetric exchange.

SikhJapji, pauri 2
Absence / relinquishmentEgoic speech is relinquished.
Appreciation / responseUnderstand hukam; walk in divine Will.
Text, practice, sources & limits

Textual basis / appreciation. Guru Nanak's Japji, ang 1, pauri 2, ends by relating understanding divine Command to speech without ego. Sant Singh Khalsa translation; walking in divine Will is immediate context in pauri 1.

Practice / response. Understanding and responsive conduct within hukam. The invocation retains Creator and Guru's grace; gratitude is not explicit in the selected line.

Our comparison. Relinquishing self-assertion is a theological comparison for responsiveness to an order one does not author, rather than an absent object to celebrate.

Interpretive limit. Hukam is not merely natural law; haumai is not a Bayesian self-model. The passage does not claim measured ego deletion, fatalistic inactivity or Buddhist emptiness.

ConfucianAnalects 15.24
Absence / relinquishmentRefrain from unwanted imposition.
Appreciation / responsePractice reciprocal restraint toward others.
Text, practice, sources & limits

Textual basis / appreciation. No gratitude addressee is specified in this exchange. Shu concerns a lifelong rule for one's treatment of another. Legge numbers this passage XV.23; Eno 2015 identifies it as 15.24.

Practice / response. Do not impose on others treatment one would reject for oneself. Reciprocity here constrains one's conduct; it does not require prior repayment by another.

Our comparison. A consequential nothing can be the imposition one refrains from making. The comparison deliberately retains a case without an explicit grateful response.

Interpretive limit. This is an ethical comparator, not an emptiness doctrine, a gratitude exercise, sacrificial reciprocity or a claim that Confucianism is simply secular.

Text, practice, sources & limits

Textual basis / appreciation. Pseudo-Dionysius, in C. E. Rolt's translation, relates praise and removal of predicates (II), placing the divine Cause beyond affirmation and negation (V).

Practice / response. Recognize limits of predication within the text's theological ascent; appreciation and exhaustive description are separable.

Our comparison. Thanks for Nothing can question an assumed exhaustive description while preserving the theological object and the limits of the analogy.

Interpretive limit. The transcendent Cause is not Madhyamaka's object of negation. Removing a predicate is not deleting a statistical hypothesis, and thanksgiving is a separate Christian textual question.
