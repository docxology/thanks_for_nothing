3. Separate optimism-task changes from predictive consequences¶

Lower Brier and log loss is better prediction. Brier combines calibration and resolution; it does not isolate calibration. The primary tests retain their existing Holm family. Paired bootstrap units are complete agents within fixed worlds, not trials or recall events.

5. Compare two explicit learning-to-gaze bridges¶

The source notebooks contain no executable connection. The primary new bridge embeds the actual learned likelihood into the declared scene. The readout comparator mixes fixed gaze templates using the assessed world-well readout. These are different mechanisms and can reverse the direction of the effect. Their transfer contrasts are exploratory pointwise intervals, outside the primary family. The gaze companion derives the policy and scene-risk score. The gaze companion also evaluates one exploratory joint region across all declared gaze contrasts, keeping these original pointwise intervals and the primary family unchanged. Joint adjustment does not remove distributional undercoverage or establish empirical efficacy.

Takeaways and next experiments¶

Preserving the original behavior, correcting the statistical design and declaring a transfer map answer different questions. Positive-looking probability is not predictive improvement. The absence condition is an exact mechanism-imposed learning null. No result establishes human gratitude, therapeutic benefit or contemplative realization.

To explore alternatives, copy the packaged GNN declarations, edit the learning or gaze parameters and pass specification_dir to the producers. Keep the original declarations and report all affected endpoints. The gaze companion and model contract explain structural access, scene changes and semantic controls.
